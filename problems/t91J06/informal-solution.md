# APM t91J06: An expanding union of arcs with the weak topology is the real line

## Why It's Hard

The space `X` is given *abstractly* by three axioms, and one must recognize the familiar space they pin down
— it is `\mathbb R` — and then build an actual homeomorphism. The conditions are easy to misread. Conditions
(a) and (b) say `X` is an increasing union of arcs `f_n(I)`, each sitting *in the interior* of the next; that
alone is consistent with several spaces (an open interval, a half-open interval, even a circle if one is not
careful). The decisive, and easily overlooked, hypothesis is (c): "a set is closed iff its intersection with
each `f_n(I)` is closed." That is the definition of the **weak (final, direct-limit) topology** with respect
to the family `\{f_n(I)\}`, and it forces `X` to be exactly the colimit `\varinjlim f_n(I)` — no finer, no
coarser. Without (c), `X` could be a different space with the same underlying set; with it, `X` is rigidly
the colimit of nested intervals, which is the standard presentation of `\mathbb R`.

## The Key Insight

After consistently orienting the arcs, condition (a) makes `J_n := f_n(I)` an *order-preserving* subinterval
in the interior of `J_{n+1}`, so `X` is an increasing union of intervals with no endpoints surviving (every
point of `J_n` is interior to `J_{n+1}`). Model this on `\mathbb R = \bigcup_n [-n, n]` with `[-n,n] \subset (-(n+1), n+1)`.
Build compatible increasing homeomorphisms

$$H_n : J_n \xrightarrow{\ \cong\ } [-n, n], \qquad H_{n+1}|_{J_n} = H_n,$$

and let `h : X \to \mathbb R` be their common extension. It is a bijection onto `\bigcup_n[-n,n] = \mathbb R`.
Continuity of `h` is *exactly* condition (c) (a map out of `X` is continuous iff its restriction to each
`J_n` is), and continuity of `h^{-1}` is the analogous fact for `\mathbb R` (it is compactly generated by the
`[-n,n]`). Hence `h` is a homeomorphism and `X \cong \mathbb R`.

## Complete Proof

Write `I = [0,1]`, `I^\circ = (0,1)`, `J_n = f_n(I)`. Each `f_n` is an embedding, so `J_n` is an arc
homeomorphic to `[0,1]`.

**Step 1 — Orient the arcs compatibly.** Since `J_n = f_n(I) \subseteq f_{n+1}(I^\circ) \subseteq J_{n+1}` and
`f_{n+1}` is injective, the composite `\varphi_n := f_{n+1}^{-1} \circ f_n : I \to I^\circ` is a continuous
injection (an embedding of `I` into the open interval `(0,1)`), hence strictly monotone. Replacing each `f_n`
by `f_n \circ (t \mapsto 1-t)` if necessary, we may assume every `\varphi_n` is increasing. Then, with
`c_n = \varphi_n(0)`, `d_n = \varphi_n(1)`,

$$0 < c_n < d_n < 1, \qquad J_n = f_{n+1}([c_n, d_n]) \subset J_{n+1},$$

an order-preserving subinterval lying in the interior of `J_{n+1}`.

**Step 2 — Build compatible homeomorphisms `H_n : J_n \to [-n, n]`.** Define `H_1 : J_1 \to [-1, 1]` to be any
increasing homeomorphism (e.g. `H_1(f_1(t)) = 2t - 1`). Inductively, suppose `H_n : J_n \to [-n,n]` is an
increasing homeomorphism. The arc `J_{n+1} = f_{n+1}(I)` is split by `J_n = f_{n+1}([c_n,d_n])` into three
order-consecutive arcs `f_{n+1}([0,c_n])`, `J_n`, `f_{n+1}([d_n,1])`. Define `H_{n+1} : J_{n+1} \to [-(n+1), n+1]`
to agree with `H_n` on `J_n` (sending it onto `[-n,n]`), and to be any increasing homeomorphisms

$$f_{n+1}([0,c_n]) \to [-(n+1), -n], \qquad f_{n+1}([d_n,1]) \to [n, n+1]$$

on the two ends. These match at the shared endpoints `f_{n+1}(c_n) = f_n(0) \mapsto -n` and
`f_{n+1}(d_n) = f_n(1) \mapsto n`, so `H_{n+1}` is a well-defined increasing homeomorphism with
`H_{n+1}|_{J_n} = H_n`.

**Step 3 — Assemble `h : X \to \mathbb R`.** By (b), `X = \bigcup_n J_n`; define `h(x) = H_n(x)` for any `n`
with `x \in J_n`. The compatibility `H_{n+1}|_{J_n} = H_n` makes `h` well defined. It is a bijection of `X`
onto `\bigcup_n [-n, n] = \mathbb R` (each `H_n` is a bijection `J_n \to [-n,n]`, and the targets exhaust
`\mathbb R`).

**Step 4 — `h` is a homeomorphism.**

- *`h` continuous.* By condition (c), a subset `C \subseteq X` is closed iff `C \cap J_n` is closed for every
  `n`. For closed `D \subseteq \mathbb R`, `h^{-1}(D) \cap J_n = H_n^{-1}(D \cap [-n,n])` is closed in `J_n`
  (`H_n` is continuous); hence `h^{-1}(D)` is closed in `X`. So `h` is continuous.
- *`h^{-1}` continuous.* `\mathbb R` is compactly generated and the `[-n,n]` are a cofinal family of compact
  sets, so a set `C \subseteq \mathbb R` is closed iff `C \cap [-n,n]` is closed for every `n`. For closed
  `E \subseteq X`, `(h^{-1})^{-1}(E) \cap [-n,n] = h(E \cap J_n) = H_n(E \cap J_n)` is closed in `[-n,n]`
  (`H_n` is a homeomorphism and `E \cap J_n` is closed in `J_n`). Hence `h(E)` is closed in `\mathbb R`, i.e.
  `h^{-1}` is continuous.

Therefore `h` is a homeomorphism, and

$$\boxed{\ X \cong \mathbb R.\ }\qquad\blacksquare$$

**Remark.** Condition (c) is the **weak (direct-limit) topology**, the "official name" the solution note was
reaching for: it makes `X = \varinjlim f_n(I)`. Conditions (a),(b) present this colimit as nested arcs filling
out a line, and "in the **interior** of the next" is exactly what prevents any endpoint or boundary from
surviving — every point is interior to some arc, so `X` is a boundaryless `1`-manifold. Conceptually one can
finish by the **classification of `1`-manifolds**: `X` is a connected, Hausdorff, second-countable `1`-manifold
without boundary, and it is non-compact (an increasing union of *proper* sub-arcs, each in the next's interior,
cannot stabilize), so `X \cong \mathbb R` — the unique such manifold. The circle `S^1` is excluded precisely
because it is compact (and is not an increasing union of proper arcs nested in interiors); the interval `[0,1]`
and half-line `[0,\infty)` are excluded because they have boundary points, which (a) forbids.
