# APM t95J02: The Klein bottle retracts onto one core circle but not the other — a torsion obstruction

## Why It's Hard

The two circles `A, B` in the Klein bottle look symmetric in the square picture (each is an edge-loop), so it
is surprising that one is a retract and the other is not. The asymmetry is invisible to the eye and lives in
**homology**: in `H_1(K)\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2`, the two edge-loops represent *different kinds* of
classes — one has **infinite order** (free), the other has **order 2** (torsion) — and this is exactly what
decides retractability.

- **(a)** Building a retraction onto the free circle `A` requires an honest geometric construction (you must
  produce a map, not just argue it could exist). The clean realization is that `K` fibers over `A`, and
  collapsing the fibers retracts `K` onto a section circle.
- **(b)** Ruling out a retraction onto `B` cannot be done by "trying and failing"; it needs an **algebraic
  obstruction**. A retraction would split `\pi_1(B)=\mathbb Z` off `\pi_1(K)`, giving a homomorphism `r_*:
  \pi_1(K)\to\mathbb Z` with `r_*([B])=1`. But the Klein-bottle relation forces `2\,r_*([B])=0`, so
  `r_*([B])=0\ne1` — impossible. Equivalently, `[B]` is `2`-torsion in `H_1(K)`, and torsion cannot map to a
  generator of `\mathbb Z`.

The subtlety is matching each circle to its homological role and seeing that "order 2" is a hard, not
heuristic, barrier.

## The Key Insight

Present `\pi_1(K)=\langle a,b \mid bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\rangle` (Klein bottle as the mapping torus of a reflection;
`a` = fiber circle `=B`, `b` = base circle `=A`). Abelianizing the relation gives `2a=0`, so in
`H_1(K)=\mathbb Z\langle b\rangle\oplus(\mathbb Z/2)\langle a\rangle` the circle `A=b` is **free** and `B=a`
is **`2`-torsion**.

- **(a)** `K=S^1\times[0,1]/(z,0)\sim(\bar z,1)`; collapsing the `S^1`-fiber, `r(z,t)=(1,t)`, retracts `K`
  onto the base circle `A=\{1\}\times[0,1]/\!\sim`.
- **(b)** A retraction onto `B` would give `r_*:\pi_1(K)\to\pi_1(B)=\mathbb Z` with `r_*(a)=1`; but applying
  `r_*` to `bab^{-1}a=1` yields `2\,r_*(a)=0`, so `r_*(a)=0\ne1`. No retraction.

## Complete Proof

Model the Klein bottle as the mapping torus of the reflection `c:S^1\to S^1`, `c(z)=\bar z`:
$$
K = S^1\times[0,1]\big/(z,0)\sim(\bar z,1).
$$
The two edge-circles of the standard square are:

- the **fiber** circle `B = S^1\times\{0\}` (the `z`-direction), and
- the **base** circle `A = \{1\}\times[0,1]/(1,0)\sim(1,1)` (the `t`-direction through the reflection's fixed
  point `z=1`, since `\bar1=1`).

Their classes generate `\pi_1(K)=\langle a,b\mid bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\rangle` with `a=[B]`, `b=[A]`; abelianizing
`bab^{-1}a^{-1}\cdot a^2`… more simply, the relation `bab^{-1}=a^{-1}` abelianizes to `a=-a`, i.e. `2a=0`, so
$$
H_1(K) \cong \mathbb Z\langle b\rangle \oplus (\mathbb Z/2)\langle a\rangle,
$$
with `[A]=b` of infinite order and `[B]=a` of order `2`.

### (a) A retraction onto `A`

Define
$$
r:K\to A, \qquad r(z,t) = (1,t).
$$
**Well defined:** the only identification in `K` is `(z,0)\sim(\bar z,1)`, and `r` respects it:
`r(z,0)=(1,0)` while `r(\bar z,1)=(1,1)`, and in `A` we have `(1,0)\sim(1,1)`. So `r` descends to `K`. It is
continuous (induced by the continuous `(z,t)\mapsto(1,t)` on the square, compatible with the quotient).

**Restricts to the identity on `A`:** a point of `A` has the form `(1,t)`, and `r(1,t)=(1,t)`. Hence
`r|_A=\mathrm{id}_A`.

Therefore `r` is a **retraction** of `K` onto `A`. (Geometrically, `r` is the mapping-torus projection that
collapses each fiber circle `S^1\times\{t\}` to the fixed point `1`, leaving the base circle `A` fixed.) ∎

### (b) No retraction onto `B`

Suppose, for contradiction, that `r:K\to B` is a retraction, with inclusion `i:B\hookrightarrow K`. Then
`r\circ i=\mathrm{id}_B`, so on fundamental groups (`\pi_1(B)\cong\mathbb Z`, generated by a loop whose image
under `i_*` is `a=[B]`),
$$
r_*\circ i_* = \mathrm{id}_{\pi_1(B)} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad r_*(a) = 1 \in \mathbb Z=\pi_1(B). \tag{$\dagger$}
$$
Now `r_*:\pi_1(K)\to\mathbb Z` is a homomorphism, so it must respect the defining relation `bab^{-1}a=1` of
`\pi_1(K)` (here `bab^{-1}=a^{-1}` rewritten as `bab^{-1}a=1`). Since `\mathbb Z` is abelian,
$$
0 = r_*(bab^{-1}a) = r_*(b) + r_*(a) - r_*(b) + r_*(a) = 2\,r_*(a).
$$
Hence `2\,r_*(a)=0` in `\mathbb Z`, which forces `r_*(a)=0`. This contradicts `(\dagger)`, where `r_*(a)=1`.

Therefore no retraction `r:K\to B` exists. ∎

*(Equivalently, via homology: a retraction would make `i_*:H_1(B)=\mathbb Z\to H_1(K)` a split injection, but
`i_*` sends the generator to the order-`2` element `[B]=a\in\mathbb Z/2`, and a homomorphism `\mathbb Z\to
H_1(K)=\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2` hitting a torsion element cannot be split-injective — `r_*` would have to
send that torsion element to a generator of `\mathbb Z`, impossible since torsion maps to `0`.)*

**Remark.** The contrast is purely the order of the homology class. A retraction `r:X\to C` forces
`r_*\circ i_*=\mathrm{id}` on `\pi_1(C)=\mathbb Z`, so it demands a homomorphism `\pi_1(X)\to\mathbb Z`
sending `[C]` to a generator — possible exactly when `[C]` has infinite order. The free circle `A` (a
section of the `S^1`-bundle `K\to A`, with an annulus neighborhood) clears this bar and even retracts
geometrically by fiber-collapse; the circle `B` (the bundle fiber, the orientation-reversing core with a
**Möbius-band** neighborhood) is `2`-torsion — going around it twice bounds — so no homomorphism to `\mathbb
Z` can detect it, and no retraction onto it can exist. This is the same functorial principle as for the
non-retraction `S^1\not\hookleftarrow D^2`, but with the obstruction supplied by *torsion* rather than by a
trivial target.
