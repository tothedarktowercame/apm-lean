# APM t02A03: Removing a core circle fattens a handle from a circle to a torus

## Why It's Hard

The two parts are the *same* local operation — deleting the core circle of a handle — performed once in a
solid torus and once in one handle of a genus-`2` handlebody, and the challenge is to see that the local
effect is uniform and then track how it sits inside the larger space. The key local fact is geometric and a
little counterintuitive: removing the central core of a solid torus does **not** disconnect or trivialize it;
it turns `D^2 \times S^1` into `(D^2 \setminus \{0\}) \times S^1 \simeq T^2`, *fattening* the homotopy type
from a circle to a torus, with a brand-new "meridian" loop that **links** the deleted core.

Part (b) requires recognizing the handlebody as a boundary connected sum of solid tori, applying the part-(a)
fattening to one summand, and knowing that boundary connected sum is homotopy equivalent to a wedge. The
subtle payoff is a new `2`-dimensional homology class: deleting a `1`-dimensional curve from a `3`-manifold
creates a linking torus (the boundary of a tubular neighborhood), so `H_2` jumps. Seeing where that `\mathbb Z`
in `H_2` comes from is the conceptual crux.

## The Key Insight

**Deleting a core circle replaces a solid-torus `S^1` by a `T^2`:**
`(D^2 \times S^1) \setminus (\{0\} \times S^1) = (D^2 \setminus \{0\}) \times S^1 \simeq S^1 \times S^1 = T^2`,
generated by the **meridian** `m` (a small loop linking the removed core, essential now because `D^2 \setminus \{0\} \simeq S^1`)
and the **longitude** `\ell` (the `S^1` factor).

- **(a)** `X \simeq T^2`, so `\pi_1(X) = \mathbb Z^2 = \langle m, \ell\rangle`.
- **(b)** The genus-`2` handlebody is `W = T_a \,\natural\, T_b` (boundary connected sum of two solid tori),
  and `C` is the core of `T_a`. Deleting it fattens that summand: `T_a \setminus C \simeq T^2`, while `T_b \simeq S^1`,
  and `\natural` is homotopy-equivalent to `\vee`, so `Y \simeq T^2 \vee S^1`. Hence
  `\pi_1(Y) = \mathbb Z^2 * \mathbb Z` and `H_*(Y) = (\mathbb Z, \mathbb Z^3, \mathbb Z)`.

## Complete Proof

### (a) `\pi_1` of the solid torus minus its core

`X = (D^2 \times S^1) \setminus (\{0\} \times S^1) = (D^2 \setminus \{0\}) \times S^1`. The punctured disk
deformation retracts onto a concentric circle, `D^2 \setminus \{0\} \simeq S^1`, so

$$X \simeq S^1 \times S^1 = T^2, \qquad \boxed{\pi_1(X) = \mathbb Z \oplus \mathbb Z.}$$

The two generators are the **meridian** `m` (a loop in `D^2 \setminus \{0\}`, encircling — i.e. linking — the
removed core `K`) and the **longitude** `\ell` (the `S^1` direction around the solid torus); they commute.

### (b) `\pi_1(Y)` and `H_*(Y)` for the genus-`2` handlebody minus a handle core

Model the bounded region `W` (a genus-`2` handlebody) as the **boundary connected sum** of two solid tori,
`W = T_a \,\natural\, T_b`, with cores `a` (of `T_a`) and `b` (of `T_b`); then `\pi_1(W) = F_2 = \langle a, b\rangle`.
The curve `C` "going around one hole like `K`" is the core of one solid torus, say `C = a`-core of `T_a`.

**Fatten the cored handle.** Let `N(C) \cong D^2 \times S^1` be a tubular neighborhood of `C` in `T_a`.
Deleting `C` deformation retracts `Y = W \setminus C` onto `W \setminus \operatorname{int} N(C)`, and within
`T_a`,

$$T_a \setminus \operatorname{int} N(C) = (\text{solid torus}) \setminus (\text{open core neighborhood}) \cong T^2 \times I \simeq T^2,$$

exactly the part-(a) phenomenon (the meridian annulus times `S^1`). The other summand `T_b \simeq S^1` is
untouched, and the boundary-sum disk that joins them is contractible. Since a boundary connected sum is
homotopy equivalent to a wedge (`M \,\natural\, N \simeq M \vee N`),

$$Y \simeq (T^2 \times I) \,\natural\, T_b \simeq T^2 \vee S^1.$$

**Fundamental group.** By van Kampen for a wedge,

$$\boxed{\ \pi_1(Y) = \pi_1(T^2) * \pi_1(S^1) = \mathbb Z^2 * \mathbb Z = \langle a, m, b \mid [a, m] = 1\rangle,\ }$$

where `a` (the longitude of the cored handle) and `m` (the meridian linking `C`) commute (they generate the
`T^2`), and `b` (the core of the other handle) is free.

**Homology.** From `Y \simeq T^2 \vee S^1`, reduced homology adds (`\widetilde H_*(A \vee B) = \widetilde H_*(A) \oplus \widetilde H_*(B)`):
`\widetilde H_1(T^2) = \mathbb Z^2`, `\widetilde H_1(S^1) = \mathbb Z`, `\widetilde H_2(T^2) = \mathbb Z`. Hence

$$\boxed{\ H_0(Y) = \mathbb Z,\quad H_1(Y) = \mathbb Z^3,\quad H_2(Y) = \mathbb Z,\quad H_n(Y) = 0\ (n \ge 3).\ }$$

($H_1$ is generated by `a, b, m`; the new generator `m` is the meridian linking `C`. $H_2 = \mathbb Z$ is
generated by the **linking torus** `\partial N(C)` around the deleted curve.) ∎

**Remark.** Both parts are one principle: removing the `1`-dimensional core of a handle in a `3`-manifold
*fattens* that handle's homotopy type from `S^1` to `T^2`. It creates a new `H_1` class — the **meridian**
`m` linking the deleted curve — and, in a closed-up `3`-dimensional region, a new `H_2` class — the **linking
torus** `\partial N(C)` (this is the linking/Alexander-duality signature of deleting a circle from a
`3`-manifold). In (a) the whole solid torus becomes `T^2`; in (b) only one of the two handles fattens, so the
result is `T^2 \vee S^1`, with `\pi_1 = \mathbb Z^2 * \mathbb Z` and `H_* = (\mathbb Z, \mathbb Z^3, \mathbb Z)`.
The other handle `b` rides along unchanged, contributing the free `\mathbb Z` factor and one of the
`H_1`-generators.
