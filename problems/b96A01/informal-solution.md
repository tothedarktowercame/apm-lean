# APM b96A01: Finite subgroups of GL_n(ℚ) live in GL_n(ℤ), and simple ones inject mod p

## Why It's Hard

This is a four-stage tower in integral representation theory, and each stage feeds the
next: (a) the structure of finitely generated subgroups of a ℚ-vector space, (b) the
"stabilize a lattice" trick that conjugates any finite $G\subset GL_n(\mathbb{Q})$ into
$GL_n(\mathbb{Z})$, (c) a binomial computation showing the congruence kernels are
abelian, and (d) a normal-subgroup/simplicity argument that turns (c) into an injection
mod $p$. The hard part is recognizing that (c) is not a side computation but the engine
of (d): the congruence filtration of $GL_n(\mathbb{Z})$ has *abelian* layers, so it can
only contain $p$-groups — and a non-abelian simple group is not one, which is exactly
what forces the reduction map to be injective.

## The Key Insight

- **(a)** A f.g. subgroup $A$ of $V$ is f.g. torsion-free, hence free; a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis
  of $A$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-independent in $V$, so it extends to a $\mathbb{Q}$-basis.
- **(b)** Let $B'=\sum_{g\in G} g(\mathbb{Z}^n)$, a $G$-stable lattice. By (a) it has a
  $\mathbb{Z}$-basis that is a $\mathbb{Q}$-basis; in that basis every $g$ is an integer
  matrix, i.e. conjugating by the change-of-basis $P$ lands $G$ in $GL_n(\mathbb{Z})$.
- **(c)** For $K=\ker(GL_n(\mathbb{Z}/p^m)\to GL_n(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m-1}))$, elements are
  $I+p^{m-1}A$, and $(I+p^{m-1}A)(I+p^{m-1}B)=I+p^{m-1}(A+B)$ because $p^{2(m-1)}\equiv0
  \ (m\ge2)$ — so $K\cong(M_n(\mathbb{F}_p),+)$ is abelian.
- **(d)** $\ker\rho_p\trianglelefteq G$; if it's all of $G$ then $G$ sits in the
  congruence subgroup $\Gamma(p)$, whose layers are abelian (c) with
  $\bigcap_k\Gamma(p^k)=\{I\}$ — forcing $G$ abelian or trivial. A non-abelian simple
  $G$ thus has $\ker\rho_p=\{e\}$.

## Complete Proof

### (a) $A$ is generated by a subset of a basis of $V$

$A\subseteq V$ is finitely generated and abelian, and it is torsion-free (a nonzero
element of a ℚ-vector space has infinite additive order). A finitely generated
torsion-free abelian group is free of finite rank, so $A$ has a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis
$e_1,\dots,e_r$ (i.e. $A=\mathbb{Z}e_1\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}e_r$).

These $e_i$ are $\mathbb{Q}$-linearly independent in $V$: a relation $\sum q_i e_i=0$
with $q_i\in\mathbb{Q}$ clears (multiply by a common denominator) to a
$\mathbb{Z}$-relation $\sum c_i e_i=0$, which forces all $c_i=0$ since the $e_i$ are a
$\mathbb{Z}$-basis; hence all $q_i=0$. Being $\mathbb{Q}$-independent, $\{e_1,\dots,e_r\}$
extends to a $\mathbb{Q}$-basis $\{e_1,\dots,e_r,v_{r+1},\dots,v_n\}$ of $V$. Then $A$ is
generated by the subset $\{e_1,\dots,e_r\}$ of this basis. $\quad\checkmark$

### (b) $G$ is conjugate into $GL_n(\mathbb{Z})$

Let $V=\mathbb{Q}^n$ and $G\subset GL_n(\mathbb{Q})=GL(V)$ finite. Take $B=\mathbb{Z}^n$
and form $B'=\langle\, g(B):g\in G\,\rangle$. By the assumed facts, $B'$ is finitely
generated, $B\subseteq B'\subseteq V$, and $g(B')\subseteq B'$ for all $g$ — and since
each $g$ is invertible with $g^{-1}\in G$, in fact $g(B')=B'$, so $B'$ is **$G$-stable**.

$B'$ is a f.g. subgroup of $V$ containing $B=\mathbb{Z}^n$, which spans $V$ over
$\mathbb{Q}$; hence $B'$ spans $V$, so its rank is $n$. By (a), $B'$ has a
$\mathbb{Z}$-basis $w_1,\dots,w_n$ that is also a $\mathbb{Q}$-basis of $V$ — i.e. $B'$
is a full lattice and $B'=\mathbb{Z}w_1\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}w_n$.

Each $g\in G$ restricts to a $\mathbb{Z}$-linear automorphism of $B'$ (since $g(B')=B'$),
so in the basis $\{w_i\}$ its matrix lies in $GL_n(\mathbb{Z})$. Concretely, let $P\in
GL_n(\mathbb{Q})$ be the matrix whose columns are $w_1,\dots,w_n$ (change of basis from
$\{w_i\}$ to the standard basis). The matrix of $g$ in the basis $\{w_i\}$ is
$P^{-1}M_gP$, where $M_g$ is its standard matrix; this is integral. Thus
$$
P^{-1}G\,P\subseteq GL_n(\mathbb{Z}),
$$
so (renaming $P^{-1}$ as $P$) there is $P\in GL_n(\mathbb{Q})$ with $PGP^{-1}\subseteq
GL_n(\mathbb{Z})$. $\quad\checkmark$

### (c) The congruence kernel is abelian

Let $m\ge2$ and $K=\ker\big(GL_n(\mathbb{Z}/p^m\mathbb{Z})\to
GL_n(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m-1}\mathbb{Z})\big)$, the reduction-of-entries map. A matrix is in
$K$ iff it reduces to $I$ mod $p^{m-1}$, i.e. has the form $I+p^{m-1}A$ with $A\in
M_n(\mathbb{Z}/p^m\mathbb{Z})$; moreover $p^{m-1}A\bmod p^m$ depends only on $A\bmod p$,
so we may take $A\in M_n(\mathbb{F}_p)$.

For two such elements,
$$
(I+p^{m-1}A)(I+p^{m-1}B)=I+p^{m-1}A+p^{m-1}B+p^{2(m-1)}AB .
$$
Since $m\ge2$, $2(m-1)\ge m$, so $p^{2(m-1)}\equiv 0\pmod{p^m}$ and the last term
vanishes:
$$
(I+p^{m-1}A)(I+p^{m-1}B)=I+p^{m-1}(A+B).
$$
The right side is symmetric in $A,B$, so the multiplication is commutative; indeed
$A\mapsto I+p^{m-1}A$ is an isomorphism of groups $(M_n(\mathbb{F}_p),+)\xrightarrow{\sim}
K$. Hence $K$ is abelian (an elementary abelian $p$-group). $\quad\checkmark$

### (d) A non-abelian finite simple $G$ injects into $GL_n(\mathbb{F}_p)$

By (b) we may assume $G\subseteq GL_n(\mathbb{Z})$ (conjugation is an abstract
isomorphism, so it does not affect whether an injection exists). For a prime $p$, let
$$
\rho_p: G\hookrightarrow GL_n(\mathbb{Z})\to GL_n(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
$$
be entrywise reduction. Its kernel $\ker\rho_p=G\cap\Gamma(p)$, where
$\Gamma(p)=\ker(GL_n(\mathbb{Z})\to GL_n(\mathbb{F}_p))$ is the principal congruence
subgroup, is normal in $G$. As $G$ is simple, $\ker\rho_p=\{e\}$ or $\ker\rho_p=G$.

Suppose $\ker\rho_p=G$, i.e. $G\subseteq\Gamma(p)$. Consider the congruence filtration
$$
\Gamma(p)\supseteq\Gamma(p^2)\supseteq\Gamma(p^3)\supseteq\cdots,\qquad
\Gamma(p^k)=\ker\big(GL_n(\mathbb{Z})\to GL_n(\mathbb{Z}/p^k)\big),
$$
with $\bigcap_k\Gamma(p^k)=\{I\}$ (an integer matrix congruent to $I$ modulo every
$p^k$ equals $I$). By (c), each quotient $\Gamma(p^k)/\Gamma(p^{k+1})$ is **abelian**.

Look at the composite $G\hookrightarrow\Gamma(p)\twoheadrightarrow
\Gamma(p)/\Gamma(p^2)$ into an abelian group; its kernel $G\cap\Gamma(p^2)$ is normal in
$G$, hence $\{e\}$ or $G$. If it is $\{e\}$, then $G$ embeds in an abelian group, so $G$
is abelian — impossible for a non-abelian simple group. If it is $G$, then
$G\subseteq\Gamma(p^2)$, and we repeat with $\Gamma(p^2)/\Gamma(p^3)$. Inductively
either $G$ is abelian at some stage (contradiction) or $G\subseteq\Gamma(p^k)$ for all
$k$, whence $G\subseteq\bigcap_k\Gamma(p^k)=\{I\}$, contradicting that a simple group is
nontrivial.

Both horns are impossible, so $\ker\rho_p\neq G$; therefore $\ker\rho_p=\{e\}$ and
$\rho_p:G\to GL_n(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})$ is injective — an injection for every prime
$p$. $\quad\blacksquare$

### Remark

The hypothesis "non-abelian" in (d) is genuinely needed: the abelian simple group
$G=\{\pm I\}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2$ inside $GL_n(\mathbb{Z})$ has $\rho_2$ trivial
($-I\equiv I \bmod 2$), so it does *not* inject mod $2$. What the argument really proves
is that any finite subgroup of $\Gamma(p)$ is a $p$-group (its abelian layers are
$p$-groups and $\bigcap\Gamma(p^k)=\{I\}$); since a finite simple $p$-group is cyclic of
order $p$, the only obstruction to $\rho_p$ being injective is $G\cong\mathbb{Z}/p$ at
the same prime $p$. For all other simple $G$ — in particular every non-abelian simple
group — $\rho_p$ is injective for *every* $p$. Parts (a)–(c) are the classical tools
(Jordan–Zassenhaus lattice stabilization in (b); Minkowski's torsion-freeness of
$\Gamma(p)$, $p\ge3$, is the $p$-odd shadow of (c)+(d)).
