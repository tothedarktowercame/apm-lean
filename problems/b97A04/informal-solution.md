# APM b97A04: A faithful simple module over a finite-dimensional ℂ-algebra forces R ≅ Mₙ(ℂ)

## Why It's Hard

This is the simplest case of the Artin–Wedderburn theorem, assembled from three classical
ideas that must be applied in the right order: Schur's lemma (endomorphisms of a simple
module form a division ring), the algebraic closedness of $\mathbb{C}$ (which collapses
that division ring to $\mathbb{C}$ itself), and a density/Burnside argument (which says an
irreducibly-acting algebra with scalar commutant is the *full* matrix algebra). The
delicate move is (c): you must see that any element of the division algebra generates a
*finite field extension* of $\mathbb{C}$, and there are none but $\mathbb{C}$. Without
algebraic closedness the conclusion fails (e.g. $\mathbb{R}$ admits $\mathbb{H}$), so the
hypothesis "$\mathbb{C}$" is doing real work.

## The Key Insight

Let $V$ be a faithful simple $R$-module, $R$ finite-dimensional over $\mathbb{C}$.
- **(a)** $V=Rv$ is cyclic, so $\dim_\mathbb{C}V\le\dim_\mathbb{C}R<\infty$.
- **(b)** Schur: a nonzero $R$-endomorphism of simple $V$ is invertible, so
  $D:=\operatorname{End}_R(V)$ is a division algebra, and $D\subseteq\operatorname{End}
  _\mathbb{C}(V)$ is finite-dimensional.
- **(c)** For $\alpha\in D$, $\mathbb{C}[\alpha]$ is a finite-dimensional commutative
  domain, hence a field, hence a finite extension of $\mathbb{C}$ — so $=\mathbb{C}$.
  Thus $D=\mathbb{C}$.
- **(d)** Faithful $\Rightarrow R\hookrightarrow\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(V)$; with
  $\operatorname{End}_R V=\mathbb{C}$, Burnside/density makes this onto, so $R\cong
  M_n(\mathbb{C})$ ($n=\dim_\mathbb{C}V$), whose modules are all direct sums of copies of
  $V$.

## Complete Proof

$R$ is a finite-dimensional unital $\mathbb{C}$-algebra and $V$ a faithful simple
$R$-module.

### (a) $\dim_\mathbb{C}V<\infty$

Pick $0\neq v\in V$. Then $Rv$ is a nonzero $R$-submodule of $V$, and simplicity forces
$Rv=V$. So the $\mathbb{C}$-linear map $R\to V$, $r\mapsto rv$, is surjective, giving
$$
\dim_\mathbb{C}V\le\dim_\mathbb{C}R<\infty. \qquad\checkmark
$$

### (b) $D:=\operatorname{End}_R(V)$ is a finite-dimensional division algebra over $\mathbb{C}$

Let $0\neq\alpha\in\operatorname{End}_R(V)$. Its kernel and image are $R$-submodules of
the simple module $V$; since $\alpha\neq0$, $\ker\alpha\neq V$ so $\ker\alpha=0$, and
$\operatorname{im}\alpha\neq0$ so $\operatorname{im}\alpha=V$. Hence $\alpha$ is a
bijective $R$-map, with inverse again $R$-linear, so $\alpha$ is a unit in
$\operatorname{End}_R(V)$. Thus every nonzero element is invertible: $D$ is a division
ring. It is a $\mathbb{C}$-algebra ($\mathbb{C}$ acts by central scalar endomorphisms),
and $D\subseteq\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(V)$ (every $R$-endomorphism is in particular
$\mathbb{C}$-linear), so $\dim_\mathbb{C}D\le\dim_\mathbb{C}\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}
(V)=(\dim_\mathbb{C}V)^2<\infty$ by (a). $\quad\checkmark$

### (c) $\operatorname{End}_R(V)=\mathbb{C}$

Let $\alpha\in D$. Consider the $\mathbb{C}$-subalgebra $\mathbb{C}[\alpha]\subseteq D$
generated by $\alpha$. It is commutative ($\mathbb{C}$ is central and $\alpha$ commutes
with itself), finite-dimensional over $\mathbb{C}$ (it sits in the finite-dimensional
$D$), and has no zero divisors (it lies in the division algebra $D$). A
finite-dimensional commutative $\mathbb{C}$-algebra that is an integral domain is a field:
for $0\neq\beta\in\mathbb{C}[\alpha]$, multiplication by $\beta$ is an injective
$\mathbb{C}$-linear endomorphism of the finite-dimensional space $\mathbb{C}[\alpha]$,
hence surjective, so $\beta$ is invertible. Therefore $\mathbb{C}[\alpha]=\mathbb{C}
(\alpha)$ is a field, finite over $\mathbb{C}$. Since $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraically
closed, its only finite field extension is $\mathbb{C}$ itself, so
$\mathbb{C}(\alpha)=\mathbb{C}$ and $\alpha\in\mathbb{C}$. As $\alpha\in D$ was
arbitrary, $D=\operatorname{End}_R(V)=\mathbb{C}$. $\quad\checkmark$

### (d) Structure of $R$ and of the general $R$-module

Let $n=\dim_\mathbb{C}V$ (finite by (a)). The action gives a $\mathbb{C}$-algebra
homomorphism
$$
\rho:R\to\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(V)\cong M_n(\mathbb{C}),\qquad \rho(r)(v)=rv .
$$
Faithfulness of $V$ says exactly that $\ker\rho=\{r: rv=0\ \forall v\}=0$, so $\rho$ is
**injective**.

By (c), $\operatorname{End}_R(V)=\mathbb{C}$, i.e. $V$ is an irreducible $\rho(R)$-module
whose commutant is the scalars. **Burnside's theorem** (equivalently the Jacobson
Density Theorem over the algebraically closed field $\mathbb{C}$, with $V$
finite-dimensional) then says that a subalgebra of $\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(V)$
acting irreducibly with commutant $\mathbb{C}$ must be *all* of
$\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(V)$. Hence $\rho(R)=\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(V)$, so
$\rho$ is also **surjective**, and
$$
R\ \cong\ \operatorname{End}_\mathbb{C}(V)\ \cong\ M_n(\mathbb{C}),\qquad n=\dim_\mathbb{C}V .
$$

**The general $R$-module.** $R\cong M_n(\mathbb{C})$ is a simple (in particular
semisimple) algebra. It has, up to isomorphism, a **unique** simple module — the column
space $V=\mathbb{C}^n$ — with $\operatorname{End}_R(V)=\mathbb{C}$. Every $R$-module is a
direct sum of simple modules (semisimplicity), hence
$$
M\ \cong\ V^{(\kappa)}=\bigoplus_{i\in\kappa}V
$$
for some index set $\kappa$; equivalently, an $R$-module is determined up to isomorphism
by the single cardinal $\kappa$ (its "multiplicity"). Finite-dimensional $R$-modules are
exactly $V^{m}$ with $m=\dim_\mathbb{C}M/n\ge0$. (Concretely: the module category of
$M_n(\mathbb{C})$ is equivalent to that of $\mathbb{C}$-vector spaces, $M\mapsto
\operatorname{Hom}_R(V,M)$.) $\quad\blacksquare$

### Remark

The argument is the $1$-block Artin–Wedderburn theorem: a finite-dimensional algebra with
a *faithful simple* module over an algebraically closed field is a full matrix algebra
$M_n(\mathbb{C})$ — a "simple algebra." Algebraic closedness is essential and used exactly
once, in (c): over $\mathbb{R}$ the same hypotheses allow $\operatorname{End}_R(V)\in
\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H}\}$, and indeed $R=\mathbb{H}$ (with $V=\mathbb{H}$) is
a faithful simple $\mathbb{R}$-algebra that is not a matrix algebra over $\mathbb{R}$. The
general (not necessarily faithful-simple) finite-dimensional semisimple $\mathbb{C}$-algebra
is a product $\prod_i M_{n_i}(\mathbb{C})$, recovered by applying this case to each simple
summand.
