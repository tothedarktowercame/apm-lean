**Stage 1 — THE CLEAN PROOF**

*Why it is hard.*  The ideal \((a,X)\subset R[X]\) looks simple, yet degree arguments interact subtly with the assumption that \(a\) is a constant nonunit.  Many students either (i) forget that a principal generator must divide **both** \(a\) and \(X\), or (ii) try to quote PID criteria for \(R[X]\) that simply fail when \(R\) is not a field.  Part (b) is also easy to miss: you must view \(K[x,y]\) as a polynomial ring over \(K[x]\), not over \(K\) itself.

*Key insight.*  If \((a,X)=(f)\) were principal, then \(f\) would divide \(X\) inside \(R[X]\).  Degree considerations show that \(f\) has degree at most one, hence is either a unit or a unit multiple of \(X\)—both cases contradict the presence of the constant \(a\).  Once this is clear, choosing \(R=K[x]\) and \(a=x\) produces the non-principal ideal \((x,y)\subset K[x,y]\).

*Complete proof.*

1. **Part (a).**  Suppose for contradiction that \((a,X)=(f)\).  Because \(X\in (f)\), there exists \(h\in R[X]\) with \(X = f h\).  Degree considerations in the integral domain \(R[X]\) show \(\deg f \le 1\).
   - *Case \(\deg f = 0\).*  Then \(f=c\in R\setminus\{0\}\).  From \(X = c h\) we deduce \(\deg h = 1\); write \(h = dX + e\).  Comparing constant terms in \(X = c(dX+e)\) gives \(c e = 0\), so \(e=0\).  Comparing coefficients of \(X\) gives \(1 = c d\), forcing \(c\) to be a unit.  But a unit generator would make \((a,X)=R[X]\), contradicting the fact that \(a\) is a nonunit.  Hence this case is impossible.
   - *Case \(\deg f = 1\).*  Write \(f = bX + c\) with \(b\neq 0\).  Since \(X = f h\) and \(\deg X = 1\), the multiplier \(h\) must have degree \(0\), say \(h=d\in R\setminus\{0\}\).  Thus
     \[
       X = (bX + c)d = (bd)X + (cd).
     \]
     Comparing constant terms forces \(c d = 0\), so \(c = 0\).  Then \((1 - bd)X = 0\), and the integral-domain hypothesis gives \(bd = 1\), i.e. \(b\) is a unit and \(f\) is associate to \(X\).  Consequently \((f) = (X)\), which contains only polynomials with zero constant term.  Since \(a\) is a nonzero constant, \(a\notin (X)\), contradicting \((a,X)=(f)\).

   Both cases lead to contradictions; hence \((a,X)\) is not principal.

2. **Part (b).**  Let \(K\) be a field.  View \(K[x,y]\) as the polynomial ring \((K[x])[y]\).  The element \(x\in K[x]\) is nonzero and noninvertible (degree \(1\)).  Applying part (a) with \(R=K[x]\), \(a=x\), and the second indeterminate renamed \(y\) shows that the ideal \((x,y)\subset K[x,y]\) is not principal.  Therefore \(K[x,y]\) cannot be a PID.

*Proof sanity check.*  The delicate step in part (a) is the constant-term comparison when \(X = (bX+c)d\).  This uses both the integral-domain hypothesis (to infer \(cd=0\Rightarrow c=0\)) and the fact that \(X\) has zero constant term.  Parts (a) and (b) are completely proved—no steps were deferred.
