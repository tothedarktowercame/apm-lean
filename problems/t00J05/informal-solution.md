# Informal solution

An essential embedded circle in a torus represents a primitive class in
\(\pi_1(T^2)=\mathbb Z^2\). After a torus homeomorphism, its class is the
first standard generator (a). Attaching the disk imposes (a=1), so the
standard CW presentation becomes one vertex, the surviving circle (b), and
a (2)-cell whose attaching map is now constant. Hence
\[
 X\simeq S^1\vee S^2.
\]

The universal cover of (X) is the cylinder (S^1\times\mathbb R) with a disk
capping each circle (S^1\times\{n\}), (n\in\mathbb Z); the deck generator
shifts by one unit. It is homotopy equivalent to a line with one (2)-sphere
attached at every integer, an infinite necklace of spheres, but it is not
homeomorphic to that necklace: (X) is locally two-dimensional everywhere, so a
line segment cannot cover it.

Under the displayed homotopy equivalence, let (i:S^2\to X) include the
sphere summand and let (q:X\to S^2) collapse the circle summand. Then
\(q\circ i=\operatorname{id}_{S^2}\). If either (i) or (q) were
null-homotopic, their composite would be null-homotopic, contrary to the
nonzero degree of the identity of (S^2). Thus both requested maps are
non-null-homotopic.
