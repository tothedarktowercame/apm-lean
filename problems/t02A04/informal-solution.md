# APM t02A04: Every self-map of ℝP² (a 2-cell glued to a solid torus) has a fixed point — via Lefschetz

## Why It's Hard

"Every map `f : X \to X` has a fixed point" is a statement about *all* continuous self-maps at once, so no
explicit construction can prove it — it demands a global invariant. The tool is the **Lefschetz fixed point
theorem**: if the Lefschetz number `L(f) \ne 0`, then `f` has a fixed point. The difficulty is threefold:

- **Identify the space.** `X` is described geometrically (a curve `\gamma` on `\partial(D^2\times S^1)`
  wrapping the hole twice and around once, with a disk glued on). One must translate this into the
  **homotopy/homology type**: the solid torus retracts to its core circle, `\gamma` becomes a *degree-2*
  loop there (its meridian part dies inside the solid torus), and gluing the disk realizes `X \simeq
  \mathbb{RP}^2`. Misreading the winding number (degree of the attaching map) changes the answer entirely.
- **Compute homology correctly.** The crux is that `X` has the **rational homology of a point**:
  `H_*(X;\mathbb Q) = \mathbb Q` in degree `0` and `0` above. The integral `H_1 = \mathbb Z/2` is *torsion*
  and **vanishes rationally**, and `H_2 = 0` because `\mathbb{RP}^2` is non-orientable. Forgetting that
  rational coefficients kill the `\mathbb Z/2` is the classic error — with `\mathbb Z/2` coefficients
  Lefschetz does not directly apply.
- **The punch line is uniform.** Because higher rational homology vanishes, *every* `f` has the **same**
  Lefschetz number `L(f) = 1 \ne 0`, with no case analysis — but one must justify that `f_*` is the identity
  on `H_0` (connectedness) and zero elsewhere (the groups are zero).

## The Key Insight

The solid torus `D^2\times S^1` deformation-retracts onto its core `S^1`, and `\gamma` retracts to a loop of
**degree 2** there (the meridian is null-homotopic in `D^2\times S^1`). So
$$
X \simeq S^1 \cup_{z\mapsto z^2} e^2 = \mathbb{RP}^2,
$$
a 2-cell attached to a circle by the degree-2 map. Its rational homology is that of a point:
`H_0(X;\mathbb Q) = \mathbb Q`, `H_1 = H_2 = 0` (the integral `H_1 = \mathbb Z/2` is torsion, `H_2 = 0` by
non-orientability). Hence for **any** `f : X \to X`,
$$
L(f) = \underbrace{\operatorname{tr}(f_* \mid H_0)}_{=1} - \underbrace{\operatorname{tr}(f_*\mid H_1)}_{=0} + \underbrace{\operatorname{tr}(f_*\mid H_2)}_{=0} = 1 \ne 0,
$$
so by the Lefschetz fixed point theorem `f` has a fixed point.

## Complete Proof

### Step 1: the homotopy type of `X`

The solid torus `D^2\times S^1` deformation-retracts onto its core circle `C = \{0\}\times S^1`, so it is
homotopy equivalent to `S^1`, with `\pi_1(D^2\times S^1) = \mathbb Z` generated by the longitude `\lambda`
(the core direction). On the boundary torus `\partial(D^2\times S^1) = S^1\times S^1`, the **meridian** `\mu`
(the boundary of a disk `D^2\times\{pt\}`) bounds that disk, so `\mu` is null-homotopic *in the solid torus*.

The curve `\gamma` wraps "around the hole two full times" — twice in the longitude direction — and "around
the back" once — once in the meridian direction; as a `(1,2)` curve `\gamma = \mu\,\lambda^2` on the
boundary, it is simple (`\gcd(1,2)=1`). Pushed into the solid torus, the meridian dies, so
$$
[\gamma] = \lambda^2 \in \pi_1(D^2\times S^1) = \mathbb Z,
$$
a loop of **degree 2** on the core circle. Attaching a 2-cell is invariant under homotopy of the underlying
space and the attaching map, so under the retraction `D^2\times S^1\simeq S^1`,
$$
X = (D^2\times S^1)\cup_\gamma e^2 \;\simeq\; S^1 \cup_{(z\mapsto z^2)} e^2 = \mathbb{RP}^2,
$$
the standard CW model of the projective plane (a disk attached to a circle by the degree-2 map).

### Step 2: rational homology of `X`

Give `X` (up to homotopy) the cellular chain complex of `\mathbb{RP}^2`, with one cell in each dimension
`0,1,2` and boundary map `\partial_2(e^2) = 2\,e^1` (degree `2` attaching map), `\partial_1 = 0`:
$$
0 \to \mathbb Z\langle e^2\rangle \xrightarrow{\ \times 2\ } \mathbb Z\langle e^1\rangle \xrightarrow{\ 0\ } \mathbb Z\langle e^0\rangle \to 0 .
$$
Hence the **integral** homology is
$$
H_0(X) = \mathbb Z, \qquad H_1(X) = \mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z, \qquad H_2(X) = \ker(\times 2) = 0 .
$$
Tensoring with `\mathbb Q` (equivalently, taking homology with rational coefficients) kills the torsion:
$$
H_0(X;\mathbb Q) = \mathbb Q, \qquad H_1(X;\mathbb Q) = 0, \qquad H_n(X;\mathbb Q) = 0 \ (n\ge 1).
$$
So `X` is a **rational homology point**.

### Step 3: the Lefschetz number is `1` for every `f`

`X` is a compact, finite CW complex, so the **Lefschetz fixed point theorem** applies: for continuous
`f : X\to X`, if
$$
L(f) := \sum_{n\ge 0} (-1)^n \operatorname{tr}\big(f_* : H_n(X;\mathbb Q)\to H_n(X;\mathbb Q)\big) \ne 0,
$$
then `f` has a fixed point. Compute `L(f)` using Step 2:

- On `H_0(X;\mathbb Q) = \mathbb Q`: since `X` is path-connected, `f_*` is the identity (the generator is the
  class of a point, and `f_*[pt] = [f(pt)] = [pt]` as all points are homologous). So
  `\operatorname{tr}(f_*\mid H_0) = 1`.
- On `H_n(X;\mathbb Q) = 0` for `n\ge 1`: `f_*` is the zero map, so its trace is `0`.

Therefore, for **every** continuous `f : X\to X`,
$$
L(f) = 1 - 0 + 0 = 1 \ne 0 .
$$

### Conclusion

Since `L(f) = 1 \ne 0` for every continuous self-map `f`, the Lefschetz fixed point theorem guarantees that
**every** `f : X \to X` has a fixed point. ∎

**Remark.** The whole force comes from `X` being a *rational homology point*: with no rational homology above
degree `0`, the only contribution to `L(f)` is the unavoidable `+1` from `H_0`, so the Lefschetz number can
never vanish — exactly as for a contractible space or a disk (Brouwer). The geometry only enters through the
**degree 2** of the attaching curve: it makes `H_1(X) = \mathbb Z/2` pure torsion, which disappears
rationally. Had `\gamma` wrapped the longitude only *once* (degree 1), the disk would have filled the core
circle and `X` would be contractible (still the fixed-point property, trivially); had it wrapped *zero*
times, `X \simeq S^1\vee S^2`, whose `H_1(X;\mathbb Q) = \mathbb Q` lets some self-maps (e.g. a free rotation
composed suitably) escape — there the property can fail. Degree 2 is precisely what produces the
projective plane `\mathbb{RP}^2`, the model fixed-point space whose every self-map is pinned by its trivial
rational homology.
