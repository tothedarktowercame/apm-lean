Informal proof

**Why it's hard:** The function f(z) = z/(e^{2πz} - 1) + z/2 has an apparent singularity at z = 0, but to factor through z² we need f to be both analytic on Δ and an *even* function — neither is immediate from the formula.

**The key insight:** The Bernoulli generating function w/(e^w - 1) has the well-known property that w/(e^w - 1) + w/2 is even in w. Setting w = 2πz, this means f(z) is an even analytic function on Δ (with the removable singularity at 0 filled in), so it has only even-power Taylor coefficients and therefore factors as g(z²) for an analytic g.

**Proof:**

*Step 1: f extends analytically to Δ, including z = 0.*

The function h(w) = w/(e^w - 1) has a removable singularity at w = 0 with h(0) = 1 (this is the generating function of Bernoulli numbers: w/(e^w - 1) = Σ B_n w^n/n!). The function w/(e^w - 1) is analytic wherever e^w ≠ 1, i.e., wherever w ∉ 2πi·ℤ \ {0}. Substituting w = 2πz, we get z/(e^{2πz} - 1) = (1/(2π))·h(2πz), which is analytic where 2πz ∉ 2πi·ℤ \ {0}, i.e., where z ∉ iℤ \ {0}. On the open unit disk Δ, the only point of iℤ ∩ Δ̄ is 0 (since |i·n| ≥ 1 for n ≠ 0), and at z = 0 the singularity is removable. So z/(e^{2πz} - 1) is analytic on all of Δ, with value 1/(2π) at z = 0. Adding z/2 (entire) preserves analyticity. Hence f is analytic on Δ.

*Step 2: f is even, i.e., f(-z) = f(z).*

Compute:
$$f(-z) = \frac{-z}{e^{-2\pi z} - 1} + \frac{-z}{2}$$

Multiply numerator and denominator of the first term by e^{2πz}:
$$\frac{-z}{e^{-2\pi z} - 1} = \frac{-z \cdot e^{2\pi z}}{1 - e^{2\pi z}} = \frac{z \cdot e^{2\pi z}}{e^{2\pi z} - 1}$$

So:
$$f(-z) = \frac{z \cdot e^{2\pi z}}{e^{2\pi z} - 1} - \frac{z}{2}$$

And:
$$f(z) - f(-z) = \frac{z}{e^{2\pi z} - 1} + \frac{z}{2} - \frac{z \cdot e^{2\pi z}}{e^{2\pi z} - 1} + \frac{z}{2} = \frac{z(1 - e^{2\pi z})}{e^{2\pi z} - 1} + z = -z + z = 0$$

So f(-z) = f(z) on Δ \ {0}, and by continuity at 0, on all of Δ.

*Step 3: f factors through z².*

Since f is analytic on Δ, expand f as a power series at 0: f(z) = Σ_{n≥0} a_n z^n. The evenness f(-z) = f(z) gives Σ a_n z^n = Σ a_n (-z)^n = Σ (-1)^n a_n z^n, so a_n = 0 for all odd n. Thus:
$$f(z) = \sum_{k\geq 0} a_{2k} z^{2k} = \sum_{k\geq 0} a_{2k} (z^2)^k$$

Define g: Δ → ℂ by g(w) = Σ_{k≥0} a_{2k} w^k. The radius of convergence of f is at least 1 (since f is analytic on Δ), so |a_{2k}|^{1/(2k)} ≤ 1 + ε eventually, which gives |a_{2k}|^{1/k} ≤ (1 + ε)² eventually — actually, the radius of convergence of g is at least the square of the radius of convergence of the even-indexed coefficient series, hence at least 1. So g is analytic on Δ, and by construction g(z²) = f(z) for all z ∈ Δ. ∎

**What connects:** This problem is fundamentally about the Bernoulli function w/(e^w - 1) + w/2 and its evenness — a standard fact used in deriving the Euler-Maclaurin formula and the values ζ(2k) of the Riemann zeta function (only even Bernoulli numbers B_{2k} are nonzero for k ≥ 1; B_1 = -1/2 is killed by the +w/2 correction). The factorization technique — evenness ⇒ factor through z² — is a special case of the more general principle: a function invariant under a finite group of holomorphic automorphisms factors through the ring of invariants.
