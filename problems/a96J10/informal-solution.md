Informal proof

**Why it's hard:** The function $f(z) = z/(e^{2\pi z} - 1) + z/2$ has an apparent singularity at $z = 0$, but to factor through $z^{2}$ we need $f$ to be both analytic on $\Delta$ and an *even* function — neither is immediate from the formula.

**The key insight:** The Bernoulli generating function $w/(e^w - 1)$ has the well-known property that $w/(e^w - 1) + w/2$ is even in $w$. Setting $w = 2\pi z$, this means $f(z)$ is an even analytic function on $\Delta$ (with the removable singularity at 0 filled in), so it has only even-power Taylor coefficients and therefore factors as $g(z^{2})$ for an analytic $g$.

**Proof:**

*Step 1: $f$ extends analytically to $\Delta$, including $z = 0$.*

The function $h(w) = w/(e^w - 1)$ has a removable singularity at $w = 0$ with $h(0) = 1$ (this is the generating function of Bernoulli numbers: $w/(e^w - 1) = \sum B_n w^n/n!$). The function $w/(e^w - 1)$ is analytic wherever $e^w \neq 1$, i.e., wherever $w \notin 2\pi i \cdot \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}$. Substituting $w = 2\pi z$, we get $z/(e^{2\pi z} - 1) = (1/(2\pi)) \cdot h(2\pi z)$, which is analytic where $2\pi z \notin 2\pi i \cdot \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}$, i.e., where $z \notin i\mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}$. On the open unit disk $\Delta$, the only point of $i\mathbb{Z} \cap \bar{\Delta}$ is 0 (since $|i \cdot n| \geq 1$ for $n \neq 0$), and at $z = 0$ the singularity is removable. So $z/(e^{2\pi z} - 1)$ is analytic on all of $\Delta$, with value $1/(2\pi)$ at $z = 0$. Adding $z/2$ (entire) preserves analyticity. Hence $f$ is analytic on $\Delta$.

*Step 2: $f$ is even, i.e., $f(-z) = f(z)$.*

Compute:
$$f(-z) = \frac{-z}{e^{-2\pi z} - 1} + \frac{-z}{2}$$

Multiply numerator and denominator of the first term by $e^{2\pi z}$:
$$\frac{-z}{e^{-2\pi z} - 1} = \frac{-z \cdot e^{2\pi z}}{1 - e^{2\pi z}} = \frac{z \cdot e^{2\pi z}}{e^{2\pi z} - 1}$$

So:
$$f(-z) = \frac{z \cdot e^{2\pi z}}{e^{2\pi z} - 1} - \frac{z}{2}$$

And:
$$f(z) - f(-z) = \frac{z}{e^{2\pi z} - 1} + \frac{z}{2} - \frac{z \cdot e^{2\pi z}}{e^{2\pi z} - 1} + \frac{z}{2} = \frac{z(1 - e^{2\pi z})}{e^{2\pi z} - 1} + z = -z + z = 0$$

So $f(-z) = f(z)$ on $\Delta \setminus \{0\}$, and by continuity at 0, on all of $\Delta$.

*Step 3: $f$ factors through $z^{2}$.*

Since $f$ is analytic on $\Delta$, expand $f$ as a power series at 0: $f(z) = \sum_{n\geq 0} a_n z^n$. The evenness $f(-z) = f(z)$ gives $\sum a_n z^n = \sum a_n (-z)^n = \sum (-1)^n a_n z^n$, so $a_n = 0$ for all odd $n$. Thus:
$$f(z) = \sum_{k\geq 0} a_{2k} z^{2k} = \sum_{k\geq 0} a_{2k} (z^2)^k$$

Define $g: \Delta \to \mathbb{C}$ by $g(w) = \sum_{k\geq 0} a_{2k} w^k$. The radius of convergence of $f$ is at least 1 (since $f$ is analytic on $\Delta$), so $|a_{2k}|^{1/(2k)} \leq 1 + \varepsilon$ eventually, which gives $|a_{2k}|^{1/k} \leq (1 + \varepsilon)^{2}$ eventually — actually, the radius of convergence of $g$ is at least the square of the radius of convergence of the even-indexed coefficient series, hence at least 1. So $g$ is analytic on $\Delta$, and by construction $g(z^{2}) = f(z)$ for all $z \in \Delta$. ∎

**What connects:** This problem is fundamentally about the Bernoulli function $w/(e^w - 1) + w/2$ and its evenness — a standard fact used in deriving the Euler-Maclaurin formula and the values $\zeta(2k)$ of the Riemann zeta function (only even Bernoulli numbers $B_{2k}$ are nonzero for $k \geq 1$; $B_1 = -1/2$ is killed by the $+w/2$ correction). The factorization technique — evenness $\Rightarrow$ factor through $z^{2}$ — is a special case of the more general principle: a function invariant under a finite group of holomorphic automorphisms factors through the ring of invariants.
