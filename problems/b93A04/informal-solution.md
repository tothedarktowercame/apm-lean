# APM b93A04: Composita of Galois extensions — and why a compositum of abelians is abelian

## Why It's Hard

The four parts move from a concrete description of the compositum to a clean structural
theorem, and each rung uses a different tool. (a) looks obvious but needs the
non-obvious fact that the *ring* of sums of products is already a *field* — supplied by
the "finite-dimensional domain over a field is a field" trick. (b) is the
splitting-field repackaging. (c) is the **lattice anti-isomorphism** of the Galois
correspondence, where you must keep straight that compositum (a *join* of fields)
corresponds to *intersection* of groups, and intersection of fields to the *join* of
groups. (d) is the payoff, and the subtlety is that you do **not** get $G\cong
G/H_1\times G/H_2$ — only an *embedding*, which is exactly enough because a subgroup of
an abelian group is abelian.

## The Key Insight

Throughout $\operatorname{char}F=0$, so every finite extension is separable.
- **(a)** The set $S=\{\sum_i \ell_{1,i}\ell_{2,i}\}$ is a subring of $K$ containing
  $L_1,L_2$; being a finite-dimensional domain over $F$, it is a field, so $S=L_1L_2$.
- **(b)** $L_i=\operatorname{Split}_F(f_i)$ $\Rightarrow$
  $L_1L_2=\operatorname{Split}_F(f_1f_2)$, a splitting field of a separable polynomial,
  hence Galois.
- **(c)** $\sigma$ fixes $L_1L_2$ iff it fixes $L_1$ and $L_2$, giving
  $\operatorname{Gal}(K/L_1L_2)=H_1\cap H_2$; dually $K^{\langle H_1,H_2\rangle}=L_1\cap
  L_2$, so $L_1\cap L_2$ corresponds to the join $\langle H_1,H_2\rangle$ ("$H_1H_2$").
- **(d)** $K=L_1L_2\Rightarrow H_1\cap H_2=\{e\}$; $L_i/F$ abelian $\Rightarrow
  H_i\trianglelefteq G$ with $G/H_i$ abelian; so $G\hookrightarrow G/H_1\times G/H_2$ is
  abelian.

## Complete Proof

### (a) $L_1L_2=\{\sum_i \ell_{1,i}\ell_{2,i}: \ell_{j,i}\in L_j\}$

Let $S$ be the set of all finite sums $\sum_i \ell_{1,i}\ell_{2,i}$ with $\ell_{j,i}\in
L_j$.

**$S\subseteq L_1L_2$:** $L_1L_2$ is a field containing both $L_1$ and $L_2$, hence
contains every product $\ell_{1,i}\ell_{2,i}$ and every finite sum of these.

**$S$ is a subring of $K$ containing $L_1,L_2$:** $S$ is closed under addition
(concatenate sums) and under multiplication, since
$$
\Big(\sum_i \ell_{1,i}\ell_{2,i}\Big)\Big(\sum_j \ell'_{1,j}\ell'_{2,j}\Big)
=\sum_{i,j}\big(\ell_{1,i}\ell'_{1,j}\big)\big(\ell_{2,i}\ell'_{2,j}\big),
$$
again a sum of products of an $L_1$-element with an $L_2$-element. Taking
$\ell_2=1$ shows $L_1\subseteq S$, and $\ell_1=1$ shows $L_2\subseteq S$; in particular
$F\subseteq S$ and $1\in S$.

**$S$ is a field:** $S$ is a subring of the field $K$, hence an integral domain, and it
is an $F$-subspace of the finite-dimensional $F$-vector space $K$, hence
finite-dimensional over $F$. For $0\neq s\in S$, multiplication by $s$ is an
$F$-linear map $S\to S$ that is injective (no zero divisors), hence surjective by
finite dimension; so some $s'\in S$ has $ss'=1$. Thus every nonzero element of $S$ is
invertible in $S$: $S$ is a field.

So $S$ is a field containing $L_1$ and $L_2$; as $L_1L_2$ is the *smallest* such field,
$L_1L_2\subseteq S$. With the reverse inclusion, $S=L_1L_2$. $\quad\checkmark$

### (b) $L_1L_2/F$ is Galois (now $L_1,L_2$ Galois over $F$)

Since $\operatorname{char}F=0$ and $L_i/F$ is finite Galois, $L_i$ is the splitting
field over $F$ of a separable polynomial $f_i\in F[x]$ (e.g. the minimal polynomial of
a primitive element of $L_i$). Then $L_i=F(\text{roots of }f_i)$, so
$$
L_1L_2=F(\text{roots of }f_1\cup\text{roots of }f_2)=F(\text{roots of }f_1f_2),
$$
the splitting field of $f_1f_2$ over $F$. As $\operatorname{char}F=0$, $f_1f_2$ is
separable (its irreducible factors are), so $L_1L_2$ is the splitting field of a
separable polynomial over $F$ — therefore normal and separable, i.e. Galois over $F$.
$\quad\checkmark$

### (c) $H_1\cap H_2\leftrightarrow L_1L_2$ and $\langle H_1,H_2\rangle\leftrightarrow L_1\cap L_2$

Now $K/F$ is Galois with group $G$, and $H_i=\operatorname{Gal}(K/L_i)$, so
$L_i=K^{H_i}$. The Galois correspondence is an inclusion-reversing bijection between
intermediate fields and subgroups.

**Compositum $\leftrightarrow$ intersection of groups.** A $\sigma\in G$ fixes
$L_1L_2$ pointwise iff it fixes the generating set $L_1\cup L_2$ pointwise, i.e. iff
$\sigma$ fixes $L_1$ and fixes $L_2$:
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(K/L_1L_2)=\operatorname{Gal}(K/L_1)\cap\operatorname{Gal}(K/L_2)=H_1\cap H_2 .
$$
So $H_1\cap H_2$ is the subgroup associated to $L_1L_2$.

**Intersection of fields $\leftrightarrow$ join of groups.** Let $\langle
H_1,H_2\rangle$ be the subgroup generated by $H_1$ and $H_2$ (this is what "$H_1H_2$"
denotes; it is the product set exactly when one factor normalizes the other). Its fixed
field is
$$
K^{\langle H_1,H_2\rangle}=\{x\in K: \text{fixed by every element of }H_1\text{ and }H_2\}
=K^{H_1}\cap K^{H_2}=L_1\cap L_2 .
$$
Applying $\operatorname{Gal}(K/-)$ and using the bijection, the subgroup associated to
$L_1\cap L_2$ is $\langle H_1,H_2\rangle$. $\quad\checkmark$

### (d) $K=L_1L_2$ with $L_1/F,L_2/F$ abelian $\Rightarrow K/F$ abelian

By (b), $K=L_1L_2$ is Galois over $F$; let $G=\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)$ and
$H_i=\operatorname{Gal}(K/L_i)$.

Since $L_1L_2=K$, part (c) gives
$$
H_1\cap H_2=\operatorname{Gal}(K/L_1L_2)=\operatorname{Gal}(K/K)=\{e\}.
$$
Since each $L_i/F$ is Galois, $H_i\trianglelefteq G$ and, by the fundamental theorem,
$$
G/H_i\cong\operatorname{Gal}(L_i/F),
$$
which is abelian by hypothesis. Consider the homomorphism
$$
\Phi:G\longrightarrow (G/H_1)\times(G/H_2),\qquad g\mapsto (gH_1,\,gH_2).
$$
Its kernel is $H_1\cap H_2=\{e\}$, so $\Phi$ is injective: $G$ embeds in
$(G/H_1)\times(G/H_2)$. The latter is a product of abelian groups, hence abelian, and a
subgroup of an abelian group is abelian. Therefore $G=\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)$ is
abelian. $\quad\blacksquare$

### Remark

Part (d) is the theorem "the compositum of abelian extensions is abelian" — the
foundation of class field theory's maximal abelian extension $F^{\mathrm{ab}}$ (the
compositum of *all* finite abelian extensions, abelian because any element of its group
factors through a finite sub-compositum, to which (d) applies). The proof is purely the
trio: compositum $\to$ trivial intersection of groups, Galois sub-extension $\to$ normal
subgroup with abelian quotient, and "embeds in a product of abelians $\Rightarrow$
abelian." Note we never need $G\cong G/H_1\times G/H_2$ (which would require also
$H_1H_2=G$, i.e. $L_1\cap L_2=F$); the embedding alone suffices.
