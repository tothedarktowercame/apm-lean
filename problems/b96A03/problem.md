## Problem Statement

Raw TeX source:

```tex
Let $F$ be a field and $G$ a finite group. Let $F^*$ denote the group
of nonzero elements of $F$ under multiplication. The group $G$ will be
called $F$-pure if for all subgroup $H\subset G$ and all homomorphisms
$\lambda:H\rightarrow F^*$, all $h\in H$ are such that $\lambda(h)=1$.
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{alist}
%------------------------------------------------------------------*
\item If $G$ is odd, show that $G$ is $\Q$-pure for the rational field
$\Q$.
%------------------------------------------------------------------*
\item In this and the following parts, assume that $G$ is a finite
subgroup of the group of ring automorphisms that are identity on $F$,
$\mathrm{Aut}_F(F[x_1,\ldots ,x_n])$.

Show that $g(\alpha)\in F[x_1,\ldots ,x_n]$ is a prime element if and
only if $\alpha$ is.
%------------------------------------------------------------------*
\item We say that two prime elements $\pi_1,\pi_2\in F[x_1,\ldots ,x_n]$
are distinct if and only if there is no unit $u$ such that
$u\pi_1=\pi_2$.  Also from now on we assume $G$ is $F$-pure.

Show that if $\pi$ is prime then either $g(\pi)=\pi$ or $g(\pi)$ and
$\pi$ are distinct primes.
%------------------------------------------------------------------*
\item Assume $\pi\in F[x_1,\ldots x_n]$ is a prime element. Let
$G(\pi)$ be the product of the distinct primes of the form $g(\pi)$.

Show that $G(\pi)$ is fixed by $G$. If $\alpha\in F[x_1,\ldots ,x_n]$
is fixed by $G$ and $\pi$ divides $\alpha$, show that $G(\pi)$ divides
$\alpha$.
%------------------------------------------------------------------*
\item Assume that $\pi$ is a prime element of $F[x_1,\ldots ,x_n]$. 
Show that $G(\pi)$ is a prime element of the fixed ring $F[x_1,\ldots
x_n]^G=\{f\in F[x_1,\ldots ,x_n]\pipe \forall g\in G:g(f)=f\}$.
%------------------------------------------------------------------*
\end{alist}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
```
