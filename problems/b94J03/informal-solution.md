# APM b94J03: The Galois group of x³−3 over ℚ, ℝ, and 𝔽₇

## Why It's Hard

The same polynomial $x^3-3$ produces **three different Galois groups** — $S_3$, $\mathbb{Z}/2$, $\mathbb{Z}/3$ — as the base field changes, and the difficulty is resisting the urge to treat the three parts uniformly. The splitting field is built from two independent ingredients: a **cube root $\sqrt[3]{3}$** of the radicand, and a **primitive cube root of unity $\omega$** (which manufactures the other two roots $\omega\sqrt[3]3,\ \omega^2\sqrt[3]3$ from one). The Galois group measures *which ingredients the base field already supplies*:

- Over $\mathbb{Q}$ neither is present and their degrees ($3$ and $2$) are coprime, so the group is the full $S_3$.
- Over $\mathbb{R}$ the real cube root is already there, so only $\omega$ is missing — a single quadratic step.
- Over $\mathbb{F}_7$ the cube roots of unity are *already present* (since $3\mid 7-1$, a fact one must prove), so the only obstruction is the cube root of $3$ — and whether $3$ is a cube mod $7$ is a concrete residue computation.

The trap is the reflex "irreducible cubic $\Rightarrow S_3$": true over $\mathbb{Q}$, false over $\mathbb{F}_7$, where the group is the cyclic $\mathbb{Z}/3$.

## The Key Insight

Track the two ingredients $\sqrt[3]3$ and $\omega$ separately. The splitting field is $\text{base}(\sqrt[3]3,\omega)$, and
$$
|\mathrm{Gal}|=\big[\text{adjoin a cube root}\big]\cdot\big[\text{adjoin }\omega\big],
$$
each factor being $1$ when the base already contains that ingredient.

| base | $\sqrt[3]3$ present? | $\omega$ present? | $[K:\text{base}]$ | $\mathrm{Gal}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\mathbb{Q}$ | no (deg $3$) | no (deg $2$) | $6$ | $S_3$ |
| $\mathbb{R}$ | yes | no (deg $2$) | $2$ | $\mathbb{Z}/2$ |
| $\mathbb{F}_7$ | no (deg $3$) | **yes** ($3\mid 6$) | $3$ | $\mathbb{Z}/3$ |

Over a finite field the answer is always cyclic, because finite-field extensions are.

## Complete Proof

Let $g(x)=x^3-3$, with roots $\beta=\sqrt[3]3$ (a fixed cube root) and $\omega\beta,\omega^2\beta$, where $\omega$ is a primitive cube root of unity (root of $x^2+x+1$).

### Part (a): $F=\mathbb{Q}$ — the group is $S_3$

$g$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ by **Eisenstein at $p=3$** ($3\mid 0,0,-3$, $3\nmid 1$, $9\nmid 3$), so $[\mathbb{Q}(\beta):\mathbb{Q}]=3$. Also $[\mathbb{Q}(\omega):\mathbb{Q}]=2$ (minimal polynomial $x^2+x+1$). The splitting field is $K=\mathbb{Q}(\beta,\omega)$, and since $\mathbb{Q}(\beta)\subset\mathbb{R}$ does not contain the non-real $\omega$ and $\gcd(3,2)=1$,
$$
[K:\mathbb{Q}]=3\cdot 2=6 .
$$
$K/\mathbb{Q}$ is the splitting field of a separable polynomial, hence Galois with $|\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})|=6$. The group acts faithfully on the three roots, embedding into $S_3$; an order-$6$ subgroup of $S_3$ is all of it. So
$$
\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})\cong S_3 .
$$
Generators: $\sigma:\beta\mapsto\omega\beta,\ \omega\mapsto\omega$ (order $3$) and complex conjugation $\tau:\beta\mapsto\beta,\ \omega\mapsto\omega^2$ (order $2$), with $\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^{-1}$.

### Part (b): $F=\mathbb{R}$ — the group is $\mathbb{Z}/2$

Over $\mathbb{R}$ the real cube root $\beta=\sqrt[3]3$ already lies in the base field, and
$$
x^3-3=(x-\beta)(x^2+\beta x+\beta^2),
$$
whose quadratic factor has discriminant $\beta^2-4\beta^2=-3\beta^2<0$, hence two complex-conjugate non-real roots $\omega\beta,\omega^2\beta$. The splitting field is therefore $K=\mathbb{R}(\omega\beta)=\mathbb{R}(\omega)=\mathbb{C}$ (adjoining any non-real number to $\mathbb{R}$ gives $\mathbb{C}$). Thus
$$
\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{R})=\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},
$$
generated by complex conjugation (fixing $\beta$, swapping $\omega\beta\leftrightarrow\omega^2\beta$).

### Part (c): $F=\mathbb{F}_7$ — the group is $\mathbb{Z}/3$

**$\omega\in\mathbb{F}_7$.** The group $\mathbb{F}_7^\times$ is cyclic of order $6$, and $3\mid 6$, so it contains an element of order $3$ — a primitive cube root of unity. (Explicitly, $2^3=8\equiv 1$ with $2\ne 1$, so $\omega=2$ works; the cube roots of unity are $\{1,2,4\}$.) Hence $\mathbb{F}_7$ already contains $\omega$.

**$3$ is not a cube mod $7$.** An element $a\in\mathbb{F}_7^\times$ is a cube iff $a^{(7-1)/\gcd(3,6)}=a^{2}\equiv 1\pmod 7$. Here $3^2=9\equiv 2\not\equiv 1$, so $3$ has no cube root in $\mathbb{F}_7$. (Indeed the cubes mod $7$ are $\{1,6\}$, and $3\notin\{1,6\}$.) Therefore $x^3-3$, a cubic with no root in $\mathbb{F}_7$, is **irreducible** over $\mathbb{F}_7$.

**The group.** Adjoining a root $\alpha$ gives $K=\mathbb{F}_7(\alpha)=\mathbb{F}_{7^3}$, of degree $3$. Since $\omega\in\mathbb{F}_7\subseteq K$, all three roots $\alpha,\omega\alpha,\omega^2\alpha$ lie in $K$, so $K$ is the splitting field. Every extension of finite fields is Galois with cyclic group generated by the Frobenius $\phi:x\mapsto x^7$, so
$$
\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{F}_7)\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z},\qquad K=\mathbb{F}_{343},
$$
with $\phi$ cyclically permuting the three roots. $\qquad\blacksquare$

### Remark — the bookkeeping and a comparison

The entire problem is the observation that $\mathrm{Gal}$ records *which of the two ingredients $\sqrt[3]3,\omega$ the base field lacks*. Over $\mathbb{Q}$ both are missing with coprime degrees, combining into the non-abelian $S_3$; removing the cube-root obstruction (over $\mathbb{R}$) or the root-of-unity obstruction (over $\mathbb{F}_7$) collapses the group to the surviving cyclic factor. The general principle: for $x^3-a$, the group is $S_3$, $\mathbb{Z}/3$, $\mathbb{Z}/2$, or trivial according to whether the base contains $\omega$ and/or a cube root of $a$, and over a finite field it is always cyclic. This is the identical mechanism as problem b00J01 ($x^3-7$ over $\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{R},\mathbb{F}_{13}$); here both $\mathbb{F}_7$ and the radicand $3$ play the analogous roles ($3\mid 7-1$ gives $\omega$; $3$ a non-cube gives irreducibility).
