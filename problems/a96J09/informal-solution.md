Informal proof

**Why it's hard:** The hypothesis is about the *Taylor coefficients* a_n = f^{(n)}(0)/n!, while the conclusion is geometric (about the domain D). The bridge is the radius of convergence formula combined with analytic continuation, but there's a subtle point: the radius of convergence is determined by the limsup of |a_n|^{1/n} (Cauchy-Hadamard), not by limsup |a_n|.

**The key insight:** The hypothesis limsup |a_n| > 0 (where a_n = f^{(n)}(0)/n!) implies the Taylor coefficients don't decay to zero, and in particular limsup |a_n|^{1/n} ≥ 1. By Cauchy-Hadamard, the radius of convergence of the Taylor series at 0 is at most 1. If D contained the closed unit disk, then by analyticity, f would extend with radius of convergence strictly greater than 1 (since the closed disk lies in the open set D), contradicting R ≤ 1.

**Proof:**

Set a_n = f^{(n)}(0)/n!. Let L = limsup_{n → ∞} |a_n| > 0.

*Step 1: limsup |a_n|^{1/n} ≥ 1.*

Pick any 0 < L' < L. By definition of limsup, there are infinitely many n with |a_n| > L'. For each such n, |a_n|^{1/n} > (L')^{1/n} → 1 as n → ∞. So for any ε > 0, all but finitely many of these n satisfy |a_n|^{1/n} > 1 - ε. Hence limsup_n |a_n|^{1/n} ≥ 1 - ε. Since ε > 0 was arbitrary:
$$\limsup_{n \to \infty} |a_n|^{1/n} \geq 1$$

*Step 2: The radius of convergence R of f at 0 satisfies R ≤ 1.*

By the Cauchy-Hadamard formula:
$$\frac{1}{R} = \limsup_{n \to \infty} |a_n|^{1/n} \geq 1$$

Therefore R ≤ 1.

*Step 3: If D contains the closed unit disk, then R > 1.*

Suppose for contradiction that D ⊃ D̄ = {z : |z| ≤ 1}. Since D is open and D̄ is compact, there exists r > 1 such that the open disk {z : |z| < r} is contained in D. (Because the distance function d(·, ℂ \ D) is continuous and strictly positive on the compact set D̄, it attains a positive minimum δ > 0; taking r = 1 + δ/2 gives an open disk of radius r > 1 contained in D.)

Since f is analytic on the open disk {|z| < r} and r > 1, the Taylor series of f at 0 converges on this disk (by the standard fact that the Taylor series of an analytic function converges in any disk on which the function is analytic). So R ≥ r > 1.

This contradicts R ≤ 1 from Step 2. Therefore D cannot contain the closed unit disk. ∎

**What connects:** This is the standard duality between coefficient growth and analyticity domain, encoded in Cauchy-Hadamard. The hypothesis limsup |a_n| > 0 is weaker than the more common limsup |a_n|^{1/n} ≥ 1, but it implies it (since |a_n| ≥ L' for infinitely many n forces |a_n|^{1/n} ≥ (L')^{1/n} → 1). The key mathematical fact is that the Taylor series of f at 0 converges in the *largest open disk centered at 0 contained in the domain of analyticity*, so the radius of convergence is the distance from 0 to the nearest singularity. If 0's domain of analyticity contains the closed unit disk, the nearest singularity is at distance > 1, giving R > 1. The contrapositive form of this statement gives Hadamard's lacunary series criterion and is the foundation of natural boundary results.
