# APM b93A03: Tensor is right-exact but not left-exact, and flat cyclic groups are exactly the torsion-free ones

## Why It's Hard

The five parts form a single guided narrative about how the functor $-\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}M$ interacts with exactness, and the difficulty is appreciating the **asymmetry** at its heart: tensoring *always* preserves surjections (right-exactness, part a) but can *destroy* injections (part d), and "flatness" (part e) names exactly the modules for which injections survive too.

- **(a)** is the easy half, but it must be argued via the right tool: $C\otimes M$ is *generated by simple tensors*, so surjectivity need only be checked on generators. Vagueness about what generates a tensor product is the usual stumble.
- **(d)** is the surprise and the conceptual crux. One's intuition — "injective maps stay injective" — is simply false after tensoring, and the skill is *finding the counterexample*: multiplication by $2$ on $\mathbb{Z}$ is injective, yet becomes the zero map after $\otimes\,\mathbb{Z}/2$, because the "doubling" collides with the $2$-torsion. Seeing that collision is the insight.
- **(e)** generalizes (d) to all $\mathbb{Z}/n$ and asks for the *classification*. The clean answer — a cyclic group is flat iff it is torsion-free, i.e. iff it is $\mathbb{Z}$ (or $0$) — requires both a positive proof ($\mathbb{Z}$ flat) and a uniform obstruction (the multiplication-by-$n$ map kills $\mathbb{Z}/n$).

## The Key Insight

The single example **"multiplication by $n$ on $\mathbb{Z}$"** powers the whole problem. It is injective ($\mathbb{Z}$ is a domain), so $0\to\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{\,\cdot n\,}\mathbb{Z}$ is exact; but after applying $-\otimes\mathbb{Z}/n$ and using $\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/n\cong\mathbb{Z}/n$, it becomes
$$
\mathbb{Z}/n\xrightarrow{\,\cdot n\,}\mathbb{Z}/n,\qquad \bar m\mapsto\overline{nm}=\bar 0,
$$
the **zero map** — not injective once $n\ge 2$. That is exactly the failure of left-exactness (part d, $n=2$) and the obstruction to flatness (part e). Meanwhile tensoring preserves surjections for free (part a: simple tensors generate), and $\mathbb{Z}$ tensors as the identity ($\mathbb{Z}\otimes M\cong M$, part c). So among cyclic groups, **flat ⟺ no torsion to collide with ⟺ $\mathbb{Z}$ or $0$.**

## Complete Proof

### Part (a): $B\to C\to 0$ exact $\Rightarrow$ $B\otimes M\to C\otimes M\to 0$ exact

Exactness of $B\xrightarrow{g}C\to 0$ means $g$ is surjective. The induced map is $g\otimes 1:B\otimes M\to C\otimes M$, $b\otimes m\mapsto g(b)\otimes m$, and exactness of $B\otimes M\to C\otimes M\to 0$ means $g\otimes1$ is surjective.

The group $C\otimes M$ is generated by the simple tensors $c\otimes m$ ($c\in C$, $m\in M$). Given such a generator, surjectivity of $g$ provides $b\in B$ with $g(b)=c$, whence
$$
(g\otimes1)(b\otimes m)=g(b)\otimes m=c\otimes m .
$$
So every generator of $C\otimes M$ lies in the image of $g\otimes 1$; since the image is a subgroup, $g\otimes 1$ is surjective. Hence the sequence is exact. $\qquad\checkmark$

### Part (b): all homomorphisms $f:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}$

They are exactly the multiplication maps $f(x)=ax$ for a fixed $a\in\mathbb{Z}$. Indeed $\mathbb{Z}$ is generated by $1$, so a homomorphism is determined by $a:=f(1)$: by additivity $f(n)=nf(1)=na$ for $n\ge 0$, and $f(-n)=-f(n)=-na$, with $f(0)=0$ — so $f(x)=ax$ for all $x$. Conversely, for each $a\in\mathbb{Z}$ the map $x\mapsto ax$ is a homomorphism. Thus
$$
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z})=\{\,x\mapsto ax:\ a\in\mathbb{Z}\,\}\cong\mathbb{Z}. \qquad\checkmark
$$

### Part (c): $\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$

More generally $\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}M\cong M$ for any abelian group $M$ ($\mathbb{Z}$ is the unit for $\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}$). Define
$$
\phi:\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}M\to M,\quad \phi(n\otimes m)=nm,\qquad \psi:M\to\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}M,\quad \psi(m)=1\otimes m .
$$
$\phi$ is well-defined since $(n,m)\mapsto nm$ is $\mathbb{Z}$-bilinear (so factors through the tensor product), and $\psi$ is a homomorphism. They are mutually inverse:
$$
\phi(\psi(m))=\phi(1\otimes m)=m,\qquad \psi(\phi(n\otimes m))=\psi(nm)=1\otimes nm=n\otimes m,
$$
the last using that scalars move across $\otimes$. Hence $\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}M\cong M$. Taking $M=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ gives $\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. $\qquad\checkmark$

### Part (d): the claim is **false** (tensoring need not preserve injectivity)

Take $f:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}$, $f(x)=2x$ (multiplication by $2$). It is injective (if $2x=0$ then $x=0$), so $0\to\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{f}\mathbb{Z}$ is exact. Now apply $-\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ and identify $\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/2\cong\mathbb{Z}/2$ via part (c). The induced map $f\otimes 1$ becomes
$$
\mathbb{Z}/2\to\mathbb{Z}/2,\qquad \bar m\longmapsto \overline{2m}=\bar 0,
$$
i.e. the **zero map**. This is not injective: it sends the nonzero element $\bar 1$ to $\bar 0$. Hence $0\to\mathbb{Z}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/2\xrightarrow{f\otimes1}\mathbb{Z}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/2$ is **not exact**, disproving the claim. $\qquad\blacksquare$

*(Concretely: $f\otimes 1(1\otimes\bar 1)=2\otimes\bar 1=1\otimes\overline{2}=1\otimes\bar 0=0$, while $1\otimes\bar 1\ne 0$.)*

### Part (e): which cyclic groups are flat?

**Answer: a cyclic group is flat iff it is $\mathbb{Z}$ (the infinite cyclic group) or the trivial group $0$ — equivalently, iff it is torsion-free. The finite cyclic groups $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ with $n\ge 2$ are not flat.**

**$\mathbb{Z}$ is flat.** By part (c), $\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}-$ is naturally isomorphic to the identity functor, which preserves exactness; in particular it sends injections to injections. So $\mathbb{Z}$ is flat. (The trivial group $0$ is flat trivially: $B\otimes 0=0$, and $0\to0$ is exact.)

**$\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is not flat for $n\ge 2$.** Consider the injection $0\to\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{\,\cdot n\,}\mathbb{Z}$, $x\mapsto nx$ (injective as $n\ne 0$ and $\mathbb{Z}$ is a domain). Tensoring with $\mathbb{Z}/n$ and using $\mathbb{Z}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/n\cong\mathbb{Z}/n$ (part c), the induced map is
$$
\mathbb{Z}/n\to\mathbb{Z}/n,\qquad \bar m\mapsto\overline{nm}=\bar 0,
$$
the zero map. Since $\mathbb{Z}/n\ne 0$ for $n\ge 2$, this is not injective, so $0\to\mathbb{Z}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/n\to\mathbb{Z}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/n$ fails to be exact. Hence $\mathbb{Z}/n$ is not flat. $\qquad\blacksquare$

### Remark — flat = torsion-free, and the shape of the whole problem

The problem walks through the exactness profile of $-\otimes M$: **always right-exact** (part a — the easy, generator-level fact), but **not left-exact** (part d), and **flatness** (part e) is precisely the property that restores left-exactness. For abelian groups ($\mathbb{Z}$-modules) the general theorem is that *flat $\iff$ torsion-free*, and among cyclic groups the torsion-free ones are exactly $\mathbb{Z}$ and $0$ — matching the answer above. The recurring counterexample, multiplication by $n$ becoming zero on $\mathbb{Z}/n$, is the prototype of how torsion obstructs flatness: a torsion element $\bar m$ with $n\bar m=0$ records that $\cdot n$ "should have been injective but wasn't seen as such after tensoring." This is the finite-cyclic counterpart of the contrast in problem b90A02, where $\mathbb{Q}$ (torsion-free, even flat) was shown flat but not projective; here the failure happens one level lower, at flatness itself.
