# APM t91J03: A simple closed curve in an annulus cannot wind around it twice

## Why It's Hard

A *non-simple* closed curve can wind around the annulus any number of times (just go around twice — it
self-intersects, but that is allowed for a mere loop). So the theorem is **entirely about embeddedness**:
winding number `\ge 2` is a purely homotopical condition, while "simple" (embedded, no self-intersections)
is the geometric constraint that must be played against it. A homotopy-only invariant cannot see the
difference — `\pi_1(A) = \mathbb Z` happily contains `2` — so the proof must convert "embedded" into
something the algebra can use.

The standard conversion is **lifting to the universal cover** (the infinite strip), where:

- embeddedness of the curve becomes embeddedness of its full preimage — a disjoint union of properly
  embedded **lines**, never crossing;
- winding number `n` forces these lines to be **finite in number** (exactly `|n|`) and **cyclically
  permuted** by the deck translation `T`;
- but each such line **separates** the strip's bottom edge from its top edge, which linearly orders the
  lines — and `T`, preserving top and bottom, must preserve that order, hence *fix* each line.

The clash — `T` cyclically permutes `\ge 2` lines yet must fix them — is the contradiction. The subtlety is
proving each lift is a separating line connecting the two horizontal ends (so that "top/bottom order"
even makes sense), which is where embeddedness and essentialness (`n\ne 0`) are both used.

## The Key Insight

Lift to the universal cover `p : \widetilde A = \mathbb R\times[0,1] \to A = S^1\times[0,1]`, with deck
translation `T(t,s) = (t+1,s)`. If the simple closed curve `\gamma` has winding number `n` with `|n|\ge 2`,
its preimage `p^{-1}(\gamma)` is a disjoint union of exactly `|n|` properly embedded lines
`L, TL, \dots, T^{|n|-1}L`, each running from `t=-\infty` to `t=+\infty` and so **separating the bottom edge
`\mathbb R\times\{0\}` from the top edge `\mathbb R\times\{1\}`**. This stacks them in a linear top-to-bottom
order; `T` fixes the two edges, so it must **preserve** that order, forcing it to fix every line. But `T`
sends `L\mapsto TL\ne L` (as `|n|\ge 2`). Contradiction — so `|n|\le 1`.

## Complete Proof

Model the annulus as `A = S^1\times[0,1]`, so `\pi_1(A)\cong\mathbb Z` is generated by the core circle, and
a simple closed curve `\gamma\subset A` represents `[\gamma] = n\in\mathbb Z`, its **winding number**. "Wrap
around twice" means `|n| = 2`; we prove the stronger statement that an *embedded* `\gamma` has `|n|\le 1`,
by deriving a contradiction from `|n|\ge 2`. By reversing orientation if necessary, assume `n\ge 2`.

### Setup: the universal cover

Let
$$
p : \widetilde A = \mathbb R\times[0,1] \longrightarrow A = S^1\times[0,1], \qquad p(t,s) = (e^{2\pi i t},\, s),
$$
a covering map (the strip is simply connected, so this is the universal cover), with deck transformation
group generated by the translation
$$
T(t,s) = (t+1,\, s).
$$
`T` fixes each boundary edge setwise: `T(\mathbb R\times\{0\}) = \mathbb R\times\{0\}` and
`T(\mathbb R\times\{1\}) = \mathbb R\times\{1\}`.

### Step 1: the preimage is a union of properly embedded lines

Since `\gamma` is an embedded circle and `p` is a local homeomorphism, the preimage `\Sigma := p^{-1}(\gamma)`
is a properly embedded `1`-manifold in `\widetilde A`; its components are circles or lines. **No component is
a circle:** a circle component `S` would be null-homotopic in the simply connected `\widetilde A`, so
`p_*[S] = 0` in `\pi_1(A)`; but `p|_S : S\to\gamma` is a degree-`d\ge 1` covering, giving `p_*[S] = d\,n\ne 0`
(as `n\ge 2`) — a contradiction. Hence **every component of `\Sigma` is a properly embedded line.**

### Step 2: the components are `L, TL, \dots, T^{n-1}L`, cyclically permuted

Lift one loop of `\gamma` (winding number `n`) starting at a point `\tilde x_0 = (a, s_0)\in\Sigma`: after
traversing `\gamma` once, the lift ends at `T^{\,n}(\tilde x_0) = (a+n,\, s_0)`. Thus the component `L` of
`\Sigma` containing `\tilde x_0` also contains `T^{\,n}(\tilde x_0)`, and iterating, all
`T^{\,kn}(\tilde x_0) = (a+kn, s_0)`, `k\in\mathbb Z`. So:

- `L` is invariant under `T^{\,n}` (which translates it by `n` along itself), and `L` contains points with
  `t = a+kn\to\pm\infty`. As a line, `L` therefore runs **from `t=-\infty` to `t=+\infty`** (its two ends go
  to opposite horizontal infinities, since `T^n` translates monotonically along `L`).
- The deck group `\mathbb Z` permutes the components of `\Sigma`. The stabilizer of `L` is exactly `n\mathbb
  Z` (it is invariant under `T^n` but `T^k L\ne L` for `0<k<n`, else the lift would close up after fewer
  than `n` turns). Hence the components are precisely
  $$
  L,\ TL,\ T^2L,\ \dots,\ T^{n-1}L \qquad(\text{$n$ distinct, disjoint lines}), \qquad T^n L = L,
  $$
  and `T` permutes them by the `n`-cycle `L\mapsto TL\mapsto\cdots\mapsto T^{n-1}L\mapsto L`.

### Step 3: each line separates bottom edge from top edge

Each `L_k := T^kL` is a properly embedded line joining the two ends of the strip (`t=-\infty` and
`t=+\infty`). Such a line **separates** the strip `\mathbb R\times[0,1]` into two components (a Jordan/
Schoenflies fact for the strip: a properly embedded arc joining its two horizontal ends cuts it in two),
one containing the bottom edge `\mathbb R\times\{0\}` and the other the top edge `\mathbb R\times\{1\}` —
because the line's ends are the *left and right* ends of the strip, so it runs "across" and divides bottom
from top. Call these the **`0`-side** and **`1`-side** of `L_k`.

Because the lines `L_0,\dots,L_{n-1}` are pairwise disjoint, they are **linearly ordered**: for `j\ne k`,
the connected line `L_j` lies entirely on one side of `L_k`. Declare `L_j \prec L_k` if `L_j` lies on the
`0`-side of `L_k` (i.e. `L_k` is "higher," toward the top edge). Since each line separates bottom from top,
this is a **total order** on the finite set `\{L_0,\dots,L_{n-1}\}`.

### Step 4: the contradiction

`T` is a homeomorphism of the strip fixing the bottom edge `\mathbb R\times\{0\}` and top edge
`\mathbb R\times\{1\}` setwise. Hence `T` carries the `0`-side of any `L_k` to the `0`-side of `T(L_k)` and
likewise for the `1`-side, so `T` **preserves the order `\prec`**:
$$
L_j \prec L_k \implies T(L_j)\prec T(L_k).
$$
An order-preserving bijection of a finite totally ordered set is the **identity** (it must send the smallest
to the smallest, the next to the next, and so on). Therefore `T` fixes each line: `T(L_k) = L_k` for all `k`.

But by Step 2, `T(L) = TL\ne L`, since `n\ge 2`. This contradicts `T(L)=L`.

### Conclusion

The assumption `n\ge 2` is impossible; by symmetry `n\le -2` is too. Hence an embedded simple closed curve
in the annulus has winding number `|n|\le 1`. In particular, **it cannot wrap around the annulus twice.** ∎

**Remark.** Embeddedness is the entire content: dropping it, the loop `t\mapsto (e^{2\pi i\cdot 2t}, \tfrac12)`
winds twice but self-intersects. The proof converts "simple" into "the lifts never cross," and the crux is
that finitely many disjoint cross-cutting lines in the strip are linearly stacked, so the order-preserving
deck translation `T` cannot permute them nontrivially. The same argument shows a simple closed curve in the
annulus is, up to isotopy, either inessential (`n=0`, bounding a disk) or a core-parallel circle (`n=\pm1`):
the integers `\pm1, 0` are the only winding numbers an embedded circle can realize.
