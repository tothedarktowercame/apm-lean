Without appealing to van Kampen's theorem, prove the following: if
$X=U\cup V$ is a space with $U$ and $V$ are open, path connected and
simply connected, and $U\cap V$ is non-empty and path connected, then
$X$ is simply connected.

\soln{ % somewhat hideously long and all in the Xi style, but it is a proof.
(simply connected: fact: sufficient condition: being the union of two open simply connected subsets whose intersection is path connected; Basic Topology p. 99)  \defn{ % it would be good to have more sufficient conditions for simply connectedness
\item $(X,t)$ topological space
\item $U, V\openset X$
\item $X=U\cup V$
\item $U, V$ simply connected
\item $U\cap V$ path connected
\item $X$ simply connected} 
\proof{ % see also t94A04
\item $x\in U\cap V$ & let % step 1
\item $\alpha:I\rightarrow X$ loop based at $x$ & let % step 2
\item $P:I^n\rightarrow \{0,1\}$ & let % step 3
\item $P(x_1,\ldots ,x_n)=1\Leftrightarrow 0=x_1<\cdots <x_n=1$ & let % step 4
\item $I$ compact metric space & compact metric space: example % step 5
\item inverse images of $U$ and $V$ under $\alpha$ are open & loop is continuous map % step 6
\item inverse images of $U$ and $V$ under $\alpha$ are an open cover of $I$ & every point in $I$ is mapped to a point in $U$ or $V$ % step 7
\item $\{t_0,\ldots ,t_n \in I\}$ & let % step 8
\item $P(t_0,\ldots ,t_n)$ & let  % step 9
\item $\forall k\in \{1,\ldots ,n\}: [t_{k-1},t_k]\subset \alpha^{-1}(U) \vel [t_{k-1},t_k]\subset \alpha^{-1}(V) $ &let (lebesgue's lemma) % step 10
\item $\forall k\in \{1,\ldots ,n\}: \im(\alpha|_{[t_{k-1},t_k]})\subset V \vel \im(\alpha|_{[t_{k-1},t_k]})\subset U$ & step 10% step 9 % step 11
\item $\forall k\in \{1,\ldots ,n\}: \alpha_k:[0,1]\rightarrow X$ & let % step 12
\item $\forall k\in \{1,\ldots ,n\}: \alpha_k(t)=\alpha((t_k-t_{k-1})t+t_{k-1})$ & let % step 13
\item $\alpha_k$ interpolates between the points $\{\alpha(t_k)\}_{k=1}^n$ & step 13 % step 14
\item both $U$ and $V$ are path connected &  simply connected:2  % step 15
\item $\forall k\in \{1,\ldots ,n-1\}: \gamma_k:[0,1]\rightarrow X$ & let % step 16
\item $\gamma_k(0)=x$ & let % step 17
\item $\gamma_k(1)=\alpha(t_k)$ & let ($\alpha(t_k)\in U \vel \alpha(t_k)\in V$; hyp 3, path connectedness)% step 17 % step 18
\item $\alpha(t_k)\in U\Rightarrow \im(\gamma_k)\subset U \et \alpha(t_k)\in V\Rightarrow \im(\gamma_k)\subset V$ & let (path connectedness of $U$ and $V$) % apparently ``simply connected'' implies ``path connected''? yes, it is an explicit hypothesis in the definition! Great.  % step 19
\item $\alpha(t_k)\in U\cap V \Rightarrow$ suitable $\gamma_k$ exists & hyp 4 % step 20
\item $\cdot $ path product & let  % step 21
\item $\gamma_k \cdot \gamma_k^{-1}$ pairs undo each other & $\gamma_k \cdot \gamma_k^{-1}$ can be homotoped to a point % step 22
\item $\alpha\sim \alpha_1\cdot \cdots \cdot \alpha_{n-1}$ & do the paths in order, you traverse same overall path % this should be rigorously cast in terms of the definition of homotopy % step 23
\item $\alpha\sim (\alpha_1 \cdot \gamma_1^{-1})\cdot (\gamma_1 \cdot \alpha_2 \cdot \gamma_2^{-1})\cdot \cdots \cdot (\gamma_{n-1}^{-1}\cdot \alpha_{n-1})$ & steps 22-23 %... undoing what was done before % step 24
\item each of the factors in step 24 is a loop & path product takes loops to loops % step 25
\item the image of each of the factors in step 24 is contained in $U$ or $V$ & steps 11, 13 and 19 % step 26
\item each of the factors in step 24 can be homotoped to a point & step 26; $U$, $V$ simply connected % step 27
\item $X$ simply connected & any loop can be homotoped to a point}
