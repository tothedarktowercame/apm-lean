# APM t98A02: Homology of a connected sum of oriented 4-manifolds

## Why It's Hard

The connected sum `Y = X_1 \# X_2` is a *surgical* construction — cut an open `4`-ball out of each closed
oriented `X_i` and glue the two leftover pieces along their common boundary `3`-sphere. To get `H_*(Y)` from
`H_*(X_1), H_*(X_2)` you must track two separate effects and not conflate them: what **removing a ball** does to
the homology of each piece, and what **gluing along `S^3`** does in Mayer–Vietoris. The genuinely subtle point
is the **top dimension**. A naive "the pieces just add" would predict `H_4(Y) = \mathbb Z \oplus \mathbb Z`,
which is wrong — `Y` is a closed connected oriented `4`-manifold, so `H_4(Y) = \mathbb Z`. The two fundamental
classes of `X_1` and `X_2` get **merged into one**. What forces this — and simultaneously makes the puncture
harmless in degrees `\le 3` — is the **orientation hypothesis**: it makes the top cellular boundary map vanish,
`\partial_4 = 0`, which is exactly the fact that does all the work.

## The Key Insight

Give each `X_i` a CW/triangulation structure with a single top `4`-cell `e_i` (the simplex `\varphi_i(D^4)`).
Orientability means `H_4(X_i) = \mathbb Z` is generated by a cycle using that top cell, so its cellular boundary
vanishes: `\partial_4 e_i = 0`. Two consequences:

1. **Puncturing `\simeq` deleting the top cell.** `X_i' := X_i \setminus \mathring\varphi_i(D^4)` deformation
   retracts onto the `3`-skeleton `X_i^{(3)}`. Since `\partial_4 = 0`, deleting `e_i` removes no boundaries, so
   `H_k(X_i') = H_k(X_i)` for `0 \le k \le 3` and `H_4(X_i') = 0`.

2. **The gluing sphere is null-homologous in each side.** The boundary `S^3 = \partial X_i'` is precisely the
   *attaching sphere* of `e_i`, so its class in `H_3(X_i^{(3)}) = H_3(X_i')` equals `\partial_4 e_i = 0`.

Therefore in the Mayer–Vietoris sequence for `Y = X_1' \cup_{S^3} X_2'`, the map
`H_3(S^3) \to H_3(X_1') \oplus H_3(X_2')` is **zero**, and everything splits:

$$\boxed{\ H_0(Y) = \mathbb Z,\quad H_k(Y) = H_k(X_1) \oplus H_k(X_2)\ (k = 1,2,3),\quad H_4(Y) = \mathbb Z,\quad H_k(Y) = 0\ (k \ge 5).\ }$$

## Complete Proof

Decompose `Y = A \cup B` where `A \simeq X_1'` and `B \simeq X_2'` are the two punctured summands (thickened
slightly to be open) and `A \cap B \simeq S^3` is the gluing collar. All `X_i` are closed, connected, oriented
`4`-manifolds. Fix CW structures with one top `4`-cell `e_i = \varphi_i(D^4)`.

### Step 1 — Homology of a punctured summand

Because `X_i` is a closed connected oriented `4`-manifold, `H_4(X_i) \cong \mathbb Z` (given), and the generator
is represented by a `4`-cycle built from the single top cell; hence the cellular boundary of that cell is zero,

$$\partial_4 e_i = 0.$$

Removing the open top simplex deformation-retracts `X_i'` onto the `3`-skeleton `X_i^{(3)}`. Comparing cellular
chain complexes, `X_i^{(3)}` is `X_i` with `C_4 = \mathbb Z\langle e_i\rangle` deleted. Since `\partial_4 = 0`,
this deletion removes no `3`-boundaries and changes no lower groups:

$$H_k(X_i') = H_k(X_i^{(3)}) = \begin{cases} H_k(X_i), & 0 \le k \le 3, \\[2pt] 0, & k = 4. \end{cases}$$

(Orientability is essential: were `X_i` non-orientable, `\partial_4` would be multiplication by `2` and
`H_3` would *change* upon puncturing.)

### Step 2 — The gluing `3`-sphere dies in each side

The boundary `3`-sphere `S^3 = \partial X_i'` is the image of the attaching map of the top cell `e_i`. Under the
identification `X_i' \simeq X_i^{(3)}`, the inclusion-induced image of `[S^3]` in `H_3(X_i')` is exactly the
cellular boundary of the top cell:

$$(\iota_i)_*[S^3] = \partial_4 e_i = 0 \quad\text{in } H_3(X_i').$$

So both inclusions send `[S^3] \mapsto 0` on `H_3`.

### Step 3 — Mayer–Vietoris

Use reduced Mayer–Vietoris (to handle `H_0`) with `\widetilde H_*(S^3) = \mathbb Z` in degree `3` and `0`
otherwise:

$$\cdots \to \widetilde H_k(S^3) \xrightarrow{\ \delta_k\ } \widetilde H_k(X_1') \oplus \widetilde H_k(X_2') \to \widetilde H_k(Y) \to \widetilde H_{k-1}(S^3) \to \cdots$$

**Degrees `k \ge 5`:** all terms vanish, so `H_k(Y) = 0`.

**Degree `4`:** the segment is

$$0 = \widetilde H_4(X_1')\oplus\widetilde H_4(X_2') \to \widetilde H_4(Y) \to \underbrace{\widetilde H_3(S^3)}_{\mathbb Z} \xrightarrow{\ \psi\ } \widetilde H_3(X_1')\oplus\widetilde H_3(X_2').$$

By Step 2, `\psi = 0`, so `\widetilde H_4(Y) \cong \ker\psi = \mathbb Z`. Thus `H_4(Y) = \mathbb Z`
(consistent: `Y` is closed connected oriented). The two summand fundamental classes have merged into one.

**Degree `3`:** continuing,

$$\underbrace{\mathbb Z}_{\widetilde H_3(S^3)} \xrightarrow{\ \psi = 0\ } H_3(X_1')\oplus H_3(X_2') \to \widetilde H_3(Y) \to \underbrace{\widetilde H_2(S^3)}_{0}.$$

With `\psi = 0` the left map injects with image `0`, and the right map is onto, so
`H_3(Y) \cong H_3(X_1') \oplus H_3(X_2') = H_3(X_1) \oplus H_3(X_2)`.

**Degrees `2` and `1`:** here `\widetilde H_k(S^3) = \widetilde H_{k-1}(S^3) = 0`, so

$$H_k(Y) \cong H_k(X_1') \oplus H_k(X_2') = H_k(X_1) \oplus H_k(X_2), \qquad k = 1, 2.$$

**Degree `0`:** `Y` is connected, so `H_0(Y) = \mathbb Z`.

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{\ \begin{aligned} H_0(Y) &= \mathbb Z, \\ H_k(Y) &= H_k(X_1) \oplus H_k(X_2) \quad (k = 1,2,3), \\ H_4(Y) &= \mathbb Z, \\ H_k(Y) &= 0 \quad (k \ge 5). \end{aligned}\ } \qquad \blacksquare$$

**Checks.** `S^4 \# S^4 = S^4`: the formula gives `H_k = H_k(S^4)\oplus H_k(S^4)` for `k=1,2,3` (all `0`) and
`\mathbb Z` at `0, 4` — correct. `(S^2\times S^2)^{\# 2}`: `H_2 = \mathbb Z^2 \oplus \mathbb Z^2 = \mathbb Z^4`,
`H_1 = H_3 = 0`, `H_0 = H_4 = \mathbb Z` — the standard answer. Euler characteristics obey
`\chi(Y) = \chi(X_1) + \chi(X_2) - 2` (one `\mathbb Z` lost at top and the puncture removing one `0`-cell's worth
twice), matching the general connected-sum formula `\chi(X_1 \# X_2) = \chi(X_1) + \chi(X_2) - \chi(S^4)`.

**Remark.** The whole computation is the slogan "*connected sum adds homology in the middle dimensions and keeps
a single class at top and bottom*," valid for closed oriented `n`-manifolds with `n \ge 3`. The orientation
hypothesis enters at exactly one place — `\partial_4 e_i = 0` — but it does triple duty: it makes the puncture
invisible below the top dimension (Step 1), it makes the gluing sphere null-homologous so Mayer–Vietoris splits
(Step 2), and it is what merges the two top fundamental classes into the single `H_4(Y) = \mathbb Z` rather than
`\mathbb Z^2` (Step 3, degree 4).
