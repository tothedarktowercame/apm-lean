# APM t01J03: Gluing two solid tori — the lens space L(2,1) = RP³

## Why It's Hard

This is a **genus-`1` Heegaard splitting**: gluing two solid tori `X_1, X_2 = S^1 \times D^2` along their
boundary tori produces a closed `3`-manifold, and *everything* about that manifold is controlled by a single
piece of data — how the gluing map `h` sends the meridian of `X_1` onto the boundary torus of `X_2`. The
verbal description ("spirals twice around ... then loops around behind and rejoins itself") is a homeomorphism
hidden in geometric language; decoding it into the homology class `2\lambda_2 + \mu_2` (wraps **twice** around
the longitude, once around the meridian) is the first hurdle.

The conceptual difficulty is that the two computations — `\pi_1` by van Kampen, `H_*` by Mayer–Vietoris —
both hinge on the *same* integer `2`, but it enters through different algebra: as a relation `\lambda_2^2 = 1`
in the fundamental group, and as the determinant `\pm 2` of the gluing matrix on `H_1`. Tracking which curves
die in which solid torus (each meridian bounds a disk in its own torus and so is killed there) is the
bookkeeping that must be done carefully.

## The Key Insight

A solid torus `S^1 \times D^2` has `\pi_1 = \mathbb Z` generated by the core/longitude `\lambda`, and its
meridian `\mu = \{pt\} \times \partial D^2` bounds a disk, so `\mu` **dies** inside that solid torus. The
gluing sends the meridian `\mu_1` of `X_1` to the curve

$$h(\mu_1) = 2\lambda_2 + \mu_2 \quad\text{on } \partial X_2 \qquad (\text{"spirals twice"} = 2).$$

- **`\pi_1` (van Kampen):** `\mu_1 = 1` already in `X_1`, and inside `X_2` the meridian `\mu_2` dies, so
  `h(\mu_1) = 2\lambda_2 + \mu_2 \mapsto 2\lambda_2`. The relation `1 = 2\lambda_2`, i.e. `\lambda_2^2 = 1`,
  gives `\pi_1(M) = \mathbb Z/2`.
- **`H_*` (Mayer–Vietoris):** the map `H_1(T^2) = \mathbb Z^2 \to H_1(X_1) \oplus H_1(X_2) = \mathbb Z^2` is the
  matrix `\begin{psmallmatrix}0 & 1\\ 2 & c\end{psmallmatrix}` with determinant `-2`; it is injective
  (`\Rightarrow H_2(M) = 0`) with cokernel `\mathbb Z/2` (`\Rightarrow H_1(M) = \mathbb Z/2`).

The manifold is the lens space `L(2,1) = \mathbb{RP}^3`.

## Complete Proof

### (a) The torus `T^2`

`T^2 = S^1 \times S^1`. Its fundamental group is

$$\pi_1(T^2) = \mathbb Z \times \mathbb Z = \mathbb Z^2,$$

and its homology is `H_0(T^2) = \mathbb Z`, `H_1(T^2) = \mathbb Z^2`, `H_2(T^2) = \mathbb Z`, `H_i(T^2) = 0`
for `i \ge 3` (e.g. by Künneth or the standard CW structure with one `0`-cell, two `1`-cells, one `2`-cell).

### (b) `M = X_1 \cup_h X_2`

Each `X_i = S^1 \times D^2` deformation retracts to its core circle, so `\pi_1(X_i) = \mathbb Z = \langle \lambda_i\rangle`
and `H_*(X_i) = H_*(S^1)`: `H_0 = \mathbb Z`, `H_1 = \mathbb Z\langle\lambda_i\rangle`, `H_{\ge2} = 0`. On
`\partial X_i = T^2` use the basis `(\mu_i, \lambda_i)` (meridian, longitude); the inclusion `\partial X_i \hookrightarrow X_i`
kills `\mu_i` (it bounds the meridian disk) and sends `\lambda_i \mapsto \lambda_i`. The gluing curve is
`h(\mu_1) = 2\lambda_2 + \mu_2`, and since `h` is a homeomorphism of tori, `h_*` is an element of
`GL_2(\mathbb Z)`; write `h(\lambda_1) = d\lambda_2 + c\mu_2` with `\det\begin{psmallmatrix}1 & c\\ 2 & d\end{psmallmatrix} = d - 2c = \pm 1`.

#### (i) `\pi_1(M)` by van Kampen

Take `A \supset X_1`, `B \supset X_2` open with `A \cap B \simeq T^2`. Van Kampen gives
`\pi_1(M) = \pi_1(X_1) *_{\pi_1(T^2)} \pi_1(X_2)`. The amalgamation imposes `i_1(g) = i_2(g)` for `g \in \pi_1(T^2)`,
where `i_1, i_2` are the inclusions into `X_1, X_2`. On the generators of `\pi_1(T^2) = \langle \mu_1, \lambda_1\rangle`:

- `g = \mu_1`: `i_1(\mu_1) = 1` (dies in `X_1`); `i_2(\mu_1) = i_2(h(\mu_1)) = i_2(2\lambda_2 + \mu_2) = 2\lambda_2`
  (since `\mu_2` dies in `X_2`). Relation: `\lambda_2^2 = 1`.
- `g = \lambda_1`: `i_1(\lambda_1) = \lambda_1`; `i_2(\lambda_1) = d\lambda_2`. Relation: `\lambda_1 = \lambda_2^d`.

So `\pi_1(M) = \langle \lambda_1, \lambda_2 \mid \lambda_2^2 = 1,\ \lambda_1 = \lambda_2^d\rangle`. Eliminating
`\lambda_1`,

$$\boxed{\ \pi_1(M) = \langle \lambda_2 \mid \lambda_2^2 = 1\rangle = \mathbb Z/2.\ }$$

#### (ii) `H_*(M)` by Mayer–Vietoris

With `A \cap B \simeq T^2`, the sequence reads `\cdots \to H_n(T^2) \xrightarrow{\varphi_n} H_n(X_1) \oplus H_n(X_2) \to H_n(M) \to H_{n-1}(T^2) \to \cdots`.

**The `H_1` gluing map.** On `H_1`, `\varphi_1 : \mathbb Z^2 = \langle \mu_1, \lambda_1\rangle \to \mathbb Z\langle\lambda_1\rangle \oplus \mathbb Z\langle\lambda_2\rangle`,
using `i_{1*}(\mu_1) = 0`, `i_{1*}(\lambda_1) = \lambda_1`, `i_{2*}(\mu_1) = 2\lambda_2`, `i_{2*}(\lambda_1) = d\lambda_2`:

$$\varphi_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 2 & d \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det\varphi_1 = -2.$$

Since `\det \ne 0`, `\varphi_1` is **injective** over `\mathbb Z`; its Smith normal form is `\operatorname{diag}(1, 2)`
(the entry `1` makes `d_1 = 1`, and `d_1 d_2 = |\det| = 2`), so `\operatorname{coker}\varphi_1 = \mathbb Z/2`.

**Reading off the groups.**

- **`n = 3`:** `0 = H_3(X_1)\oplus H_3(X_2) \to H_3(M) \to H_2(T^2) = \mathbb Z \xrightarrow{\varphi_2} H_2(X_1)\oplus H_2(X_2) = 0`,
  and `\varphi_2 = 0`, so `H_3(M) \cong \mathbb Z`. (Consistent: `M` is a closed orientable `3`-manifold.)
- **`n = 2`:** `0 = H_2(X_1)\oplus H_2(X_2) \to H_2(M) \to H_1(T^2) = \mathbb Z^2 \xrightarrow{\varphi_1} \mathbb Z^2`,
  so `H_2(M) = \ker\varphi_1 = 0`.
- **`n = 1`:** `\mathbb Z^2 \xrightarrow{\varphi_1} \mathbb Z^2 \to H_1(M) \to H_0(T^2) = \mathbb Z \xrightarrow{\varphi_0} \mathbb Z^2`.
  The map `\varphi_0` (induced by the two inclusions of the connected `T^2`) is `x \mapsto (x, x)`, injective,
  so `H_1(M) = \operatorname{coker}\varphi_1 = \mathbb Z/2`.
- **`n = 0`:** `M` is connected, `H_0(M) = \mathbb Z`.

$$\boxed{\ H_0(M) = \mathbb Z,\quad H_1(M) = \mathbb Z/2,\quad H_2(M) = 0,\quad H_3(M) = \mathbb Z,\quad H_i(M) = 0\ (i \ge 4).\ }$$

These are exactly the homology groups of `\mathbb{RP}^3`. ∎

**Remark.** `M` is the **lens space** `L(2,1) = \mathbb{RP}^3`: gluing two solid tori so that the meridian of
one wraps `p` times around the longitude of the other yields `L(p,q)` with `\pi_1 = \mathbb Z/p`. Here the
single geometric datum "spirals **twice**" is the integer `p = 2`, and it surfaces twice over: as the relation
`\lambda_2^2 = 1` giving `\pi_1 = \mathbb Z/2`, and as the determinant `\pm 2` of the `H_1` gluing matrix
giving `H_1 = \mathbb Z/2` and `H_2 = 0`. The agreement `H_1(M) = \mathbb Z/2 = \pi_1(M)^{\mathrm{ab}}` is the
Hurewicz check. The whole topology of `M` is read off from one wrapping number.
