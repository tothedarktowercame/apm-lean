# APM b00J01: The Galois group of x³−7 over ℚ, ℝ, and 𝔽₁₃

## Why It's Hard

The striking feature is that **one polynomial yields three completely different Galois groups** — $S_3$, $\mathbb{Z}/2$, $\mathbb{Z}/3$ — depending only on the base field. The difficulty is resisting the urge to treat the three parts identically and instead seeing *what each base field already supplies*.

A splitting field of $x^3-7$ is generated by two independent ingredients: a **cube root $\sqrt[3]{7}$** of the radicand, and a **primitive cube root of unity $\omega$** (needed to manufacture the other two roots $\omega\sqrt[3]7,\ \omega^2\sqrt[3]7$ from one). The Galois group measures precisely *which ingredients the base field is missing*:

- Over $\mathbb{Q}$ both are absent and independent (degrees $3$ and $2$, coprime) — the group is as big as possible, the full $S_3$, and the genuinely hard part is then the *entire Galois correspondence*: six subgroups, six fields, and correctly diagnosing which intermediate fields fail to be normal.
- Over $\mathbb{R}$ the real cube root is already there, so only $\omega$ is missing — a single quadratic step.
- Over $\mathbb{F}_{13}$ the cube roots of unity are *already present* (a fact one must prove, via $3\mid 12$), so the only obstruction is the cube root of $7$ — and deciding whether $7$ is a cube mod $13$ is a concrete cubic-residue computation.

The trap is assuming "irreducible cubic ⟹ $S_3$": that holds over $\mathbb{Q}$ but is false over $\mathbb{F}_{13}$, where the group is cyclic of order $3$.

## The Key Insight

Track the **two ingredients** $\sqrt[3]7$ and $\omega$ separately. The splitting field is $\text{base}(\sqrt[3]7,\omega)$, and
$$
|\mathrm{Gal}| = [\,\text{base}(\sqrt[3]7,\omega):\text{base}\,]=\big[\text{adjoin a cube root}\big]\cdot\big[\text{adjoin }\omega\big],
$$
each factor being $1$ if the base already contains that ingredient. Over $\mathbb{Q}$: $3\cdot2=6=|S_3|$. Over $\mathbb{R}$: $1\cdot2=2$. Over $\mathbb{F}_{13}$: $\omega\in\mathbb{F}_{13}$ (since $|\mathbb{F}_{13}^\times|=12$ is divisible by $3$), so the $\omega$-factor is $1$, and the cube-root factor is $3$ because $7$ is a non-cube mod $13$ — giving a cyclic $\mathbb{Z}/3$, the Frobenius automorphism.

## Complete Proof

Let $f(x)=x^3-7$, and let $\beta:=\sqrt[3]7$ (a fixed cube root) and $\omega:=e^{2\pi i/3}$ (a primitive cube root of unity, root of $x^2+x+1$). The three roots of $f$ are $\beta,\ \omega\beta,\ \omega^2\beta$.

### Part (a): over $\mathbb{Q}$ — the group is $S_3$

**The splitting field.** $K=\mathbb{Q}(\beta,\omega\beta,\omega^2\beta)=\mathbb{Q}(\beta,\omega)$. By **Eisenstein at $p=7$**, $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, so $[\mathbb{Q}(\beta):\mathbb{Q}]=3$. Also $[\mathbb{Q}(\omega):\mathbb{Q}]=2$ (minimal polynomial $x^2+x+1$). Since $\mathbb{Q}(\beta)\subset\mathbb{R}$ does not contain the non-real $\omega$, and $\gcd(3,2)=1$,
$$
[K:\mathbb{Q}]=[\mathbb{Q}(\beta):\mathbb{Q}]\cdot[\mathbb{Q}(\omega):\mathbb{Q}]=3\cdot2=6 .
$$

**The group.** $K/\mathbb{Q}$ is the splitting field of a separable polynomial, hence Galois, with $|\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})|=6$. The group acts faithfully on the three roots of $f$, embedding it in $S_3$; an order-$6$ subgroup of $S_3$ is all of $S_3$. Thus
$$
\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})\cong S_3\ (\cong D_3).
$$
Explicit generators: $\sigma:\beta\mapsto\omega\beta,\ \omega\mapsto\omega$ (order $3$, the $3$-cycle on the roots) and complex conjugation $\tau:\beta\mapsto\beta,\ \omega\mapsto\omega^2$ (order $2$, the transposition swapping the two non-real roots), with $\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}=\sigma^{-1}$.

**Intermediate fields (Galois correspondence).** Subgroups of $S_3$ correspond to intermediate fields, inclusion-reversing, with degree = index:

| Subgroup of $S_3$ | order | Fixed field | $[\,\cdot:\mathbb{Q}]$ | Galois over $\mathbb{Q}$? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\{e\}$ | $1$ | $K$ | $6$ | yes |
| $A_3=\langle\sigma\rangle$ | $3$ | $\mathbb{Q}(\omega)=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$ | $2$ | **yes** (normal, index $2$) |
| $\langle\tau\rangle$ | $2$ | $\mathbb{Q}(\beta)$ | $3$ | **no** |
| $\langle\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}\rangle$ | $2$ | $\mathbb{Q}(\omega^2\beta)$ | $3$ | **no** |
| $\langle\sigma^2\tau\sigma^{-2}\rangle$ | $2$ | $\mathbb{Q}(\omega\beta)$ | $3$ | **no** |
| $S_3$ | $6$ | $\mathbb{Q}$ | $1$ | yes |

So the intermediate fields are: $\mathbb{Q}$, $K$, the quadratic $\mathbb{Q}(\omega)=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$, and the three cubic fields $\mathbb{Q}(\beta),\ \mathbb{Q}(\omega\beta),\ \mathbb{Q}(\omega^2\beta)$.

**Which are not Galois.** The three cubic subfields $\mathbb{Q}(\beta),\mathbb{Q}(\omega\beta),\mathbb{Q}(\omega^2\beta)$ are **not** Galois over $\mathbb{Q}$: their corresponding order-$2$ subgroups are not normal in $S_3$ (the three transpositions are conjugate, not normal). Concretely, each such field contains exactly one root of $f$, so $f$ does not split there — the extension is not normal. The quadratic $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$ *is* Galois (every degree-$2$ extension is normal; equivalently $A_3\trianglelefteq S_3$).

### Part (b): over $\mathbb{R}$ — the group is $\mathbb{Z}/2$

Over $\mathbb{R}$ the real cube root $\beta=\sqrt[3]7$ already lies in the base field, and $f$ factors as
$$
x^3-7=(x-\beta)\,(x^2+\beta x+\beta^2),
$$
whose quadratic factor has discriminant $\beta^2-4\beta^2=-3\beta^2<0$ and hence two complex-conjugate (non-real) roots $\omega\beta,\omega^2\beta$. The splitting field is therefore $L=\mathbb{R}(\omega\beta)=\mathbb{R}(\omega)=\mathbb{C}$ (adjoining any non-real number to $\mathbb{R}$ gives $\mathbb{C}$, and $[\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R}]=2$). Thus
$$
\mathrm{Gal}(L/\mathbb{R})=\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},
$$
generated by complex conjugation (which fixes $\beta$ and swaps $\omega\beta\leftrightarrow\omega^2\beta$).

### Part (c): over $\mathbb{F}_{13}$ — the group is $\mathbb{Z}/3$

**$\omega\in\mathbb{F}_{13}$.** The group $\mathbb{F}_{13}^\times$ is cyclic of order $12$, and $3\mid 12$, so it contains an element of order $3$ — a primitive cube root of unity. Hence $\mathbb{F}_{13}$ already contains $\omega$.

**$7$ is not a cube mod $13$.** An element $a\in\mathbb{F}_{13}^\times$ is a cube iff $a^{(13-1)/\gcd(3,12)}=a^{4}=1$. Compute $7^2=49\equiv-3$, so $7^4\equiv(-3)^2=9\not\equiv1\pmod{13}$. Therefore $7$ has no cube root in $\mathbb{F}_{13}$, and the cubic $f(x)=x^3-7$ — having no root in $\mathbb{F}_{13}$ — is **irreducible** over $\mathbb{F}_{13}$.

**The group.** Adjoining a root $\alpha$ of the irreducible cubic gives $M=\mathbb{F}_{13}(\alpha)=\mathbb{F}_{13^3}$, of degree $3$. Every extension of finite fields is Galois with cyclic Galois group generated by the **Frobenius** $\phi:x\mapsto x^{13}$; in particular $\mathbb{F}_{13^3}$ contains all three roots of $f$ (it is the splitting field automatically). Hence
$$
\mathrm{Gal}(M/\mathbb{F}_{13})\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z},\qquad\text{generated by }\phi:x\mapsto x^{13}.
$$
On the roots, $\phi$ acts as a $3$-cycle $\beta\mapsto\omega\beta$ (where now $\omega\in\mathbb{F}_{13}$): since $\phi(\alpha)=\alpha^{13}=\alpha\cdot\alpha^{12}=\alpha\cdot(\alpha^3)^4=\alpha\cdot7^4$ and $7^4\equiv 9$ is the cube root of unity $\equiv\omega^{?}$ — concretely $\phi$ permutes the three roots cyclically. $\qquad\blacksquare$

### Remark — the unifying bookkeeping

| Base | $\sqrt[3]7$ present? | $\omega$ present? | $[\,\text{split}:\text{base}\,]$ | $\mathrm{Gal}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\mathbb{Q}$ | no (deg $3$) | no (deg $2$) | $6$ | $S_3$ |
| $\mathbb{R}$ | yes | no (deg $2$) | $2$ | $\mathbb{Z}/2$ |
| $\mathbb{F}_{13}$ | no (deg $3$) | **yes** | $3$ | $\mathbb{Z}/3$ |

The entire problem is the observation that $\mathrm{Gal}$ records *which of the two ingredients the base lacks*. Over $\mathbb{Q}$ the two missing pieces have coprime degrees and combine to the non-abelian $S_3$; removing the cube-root obstruction (over $\mathbb{R}$) or the root-of-unity obstruction (over $\mathbb{F}_{13}$) collapses the group to the corresponding cyclic factor. The general principle: for $x^3-a$, the group is $S_3$, $\mathbb{Z}/3$, $\mathbb{Z}/2$, or trivial according to whether the base contains $\omega$ and/or a cube root of $a$ — and over finite fields the answer is always cyclic, because finite-field extensions are.
