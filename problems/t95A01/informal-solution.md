# APM t95A01: Double covers via index-2 subgroups — three (non)existence questions

## Why It's Hard

All three parts are really one piece of covering-space theory seen from different angles: for a nice
path-connected space `X`, **connected double covers correspond to index-`2` subgroups of `\pi_1(X)`**
(equivalently, to surjections `\pi_1(X) \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb Z/2`). The difficulty is translating each
geometric requirement into a group-theoretic one and then either *building* the example or *proving the
obstruction*:

- (a) "no connected double cover" means `\pi_1` has **no index-`2` subgroup** — yet `\pi_1 \ne 1`;
- (b) "a double cover *of itself*" means `\pi_1` has an index-`2` subgroup **isomorphic to `\pi_1`**;
- (c) the same self-cover condition, but now with `\pi_1` **finite** — where an order count forbids it.

The trap is forgetting that for a self-cover the total space and base coincide, so their `\pi_1`'s are
isomorphic — which is exactly what makes (b) possible (infinite group) and (c) impossible (finite group).

## The Key Insight

A connected double cover `p : \widetilde X \to X` corresponds to an index-`2` subgroup `H = p_*\pi_1(\widetilde X) \le \pi_1(X)`,
and `\pi_1(\widetilde X) \cong H`.

- **(a) Exists.** Take any space with `\pi_1 = \mathbb Z/3` (e.g. the lens space `L(3,1)`, or the `2`-complex
  `S^1 \cup_{z\mapsto z^3} e^2`). It is path-connected and not simply connected, and `\mathbb Z/3` (odd order)
  has **no index-`2` subgroup**, so there is no connected double cover.
- **(b) Exists.** The **Klein bottle** `K`, `\pi_1(K) = \langle a, b \mid bab^{-1} = a^{-1}\rangle`, has the
  index-`2` subgroup `\langle a^2, b\rangle`, and `\langle a^2, b\rangle \cong \pi_1(K)` (set `a' = a^2`:
  `ba'b^{-1} = a'^{-1}`). So `K` double-covers **itself** (non-orientable, `\chi = 0 = 2\cdot 0`).
- **(c) Cannot exist.** A self-double-cover forces an index-`2` subgroup `H \cong \pi_1(X) =: G`. If `G` is
  finite, `|H| = |G|/2` but `|H| = |G|`, so `|G| = |G|/2`, impossible.

## Complete Proof

### (a) Path-connected, not simply connected, no connected double covering — EXISTS

Let `X` be a space with `\pi_1(X) \cong \mathbb Z/3` — for instance the lens space `L(3,1) = S^3/(\mathbb Z/3)`,
or the presentation `2`-complex of `\langle a \mid a^3\rangle` (attach a `2`-cell to `S^1` by the degree-`3`
map). `X` is path-connected and **not simply connected** (`\pi_1 = \mathbb Z/3 \ne 1`).

Connected double covers of `X` correspond to subgroups of index `2` in `\pi_1(X) = \mathbb Z/3`. By Lagrange,
an index-`2` subgroup would have order `3/2 \notin \mathbb Z` — there is none. (Equivalently, `\mathbb Z/3` has
no surjection onto `\mathbb Z/2`.) Hence `X` has **no connected double covering**. ✓

### (b) A non-orientable surface that double-covers itself — EXISTS

A surface double-covering itself has `\chi = 2\chi`, forcing `\chi = 0`; the non-orientable such surface is the
**Klein bottle** `K`, with

$$\pi_1(K) = \langle a, b \mid bab^{-1} = a^{-1}\rangle.$$

Consider `H = \langle a^2, b\rangle \le \pi_1(K)`. It has **index `2`**: the quotient `\pi_1(K)/\langle\langle a^2, b\rangle\rangle = \langle a \mid a^2\rangle = \mathbb Z/2`
(killing `b` and `a^2`). And `H \cong \pi_1(K)`: from `bab^{-1} = a^{-1}` we get `ba^2 b^{-1} = a^{-2}`, so with
`a' := a^2`, `H = \langle a', b \mid ba'b^{-1} = a'^{-1}\rangle`, the Klein-bottle group itself.

Therefore the connected double cover of `K` associated to `H` has fundamental group `H \cong \pi_1(K)` and is
again a Klein bottle (a closed non-orientable surface with `\chi = -2 \cdot 0 \cdot \dots`— in fact
`\chi(\widetilde K) = 2\chi(K) = 0`, non-orientable, hence `K`). So **`K` is a double cover of itself**.
(Concretely: realize `K = \mathbb R^2/\Gamma` with `\Gamma` generated by the glide reflection
`g:(x,y)\mapsto(-x, y+\tfrac12)` and translation `t:(x,y)\mapsto(x+1,y)`; the index-`2` subgroup
`\langle t^2, g\rangle` is again such a group, and `\mathbb R^2/\langle t^2, g\rangle \to \mathbb R^2/\Gamma`
is a double cover `K \to K`.) ✓

### (c) Path-connected with finite `\pi_1`, double-covering itself — CANNOT EXIST

Suppose `X` is path-connected with `\pi_1(X) = G` finite, and `p : X \to X` is a (connected) double cover.
Then `p` corresponds to a subgroup `H = p_*\pi_1(X) \le G` of **index `2`**, and since the total space *is* `X`,

$$H \cong \pi_1(X) = G.$$

Now count orders. Index `2` gives `|H| = |G|/2`, while `H \cong G` gives `|H| = |G|`. Hence
`|G| = |G|/2`, i.e. `|G| = 0` — impossible for a (finite, nonempty) group. (And `G = 1` is excluded too: a
simply connected space has no connected double cover, only the disconnected `X \sqcup X`.) Therefore **no such
space exists**. ✓

$$\boxed{\ \text{(a) exists } (\pi_1 = \mathbb Z/3);\quad \text{(b) exists (Klein bottle)};\quad \text{(c) impossible (order count).}\ }\qquad\blacksquare$$

**Remark.** The single organizing fact is: a connected double self-cover means `\pi_1` contains an index-`2`
subgroup *isomorphic to itself*. Infinite groups can do this — the Klein-bottle group `\langle a,b\mid bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\rangle`
"absorbs" the index by replacing `a` with `a^2` — which is why a non-orientable surface can cover itself.
Finite groups cannot, by the order count `|G| = |G|/2`; this is the same reason a *finite-sheeted* self-cover
of any space with finite `\pi_1` is impossible beyond the trivial one. And "no double cover at all" (part a)
is the orthogonal condition that `\pi_1` has no index-`2` subgroup whatsoever, realized by any group of odd
order such as `\mathbb Z/3`.
