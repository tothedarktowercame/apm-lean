# APM t00A04: A retraction prevents the inclusion from being null-homotopic

## Why It's Hard

The hypothesis `f|_X = \mathrm{id}_X` says `f : Y \to X` is a **retraction** of `Y` onto the submanifold
`X`, i.e. `f \circ \iota = \mathrm{id}_X`. The conclusion — that the inclusion `\iota : X \hookrightarrow Y`
cannot be deformed to a constant map — is a statement about *homotopy*, which is hard to attack directly:
one cannot easily rule out every conceivable homotopy by hand. The standard resolution is to pass to an
**algebraic invariant** that homotopy must preserve, and to play two facts against each other: a retraction
forces the induced map on the invariant to be *injective*, while a null-homotopy forces it to be *zero*.
The contradiction only bites if `X` actually carries a nonzero invariant in positive degree — and that is
exactly where the hypotheses "compact, oriented, without boundary" earn their place: they guarantee `X` has
a nonzero **top homology class** (its fundamental class), the obstruction that makes the argument work.

## The Key Insight

Apply a functor — singular homology `H_*(\cdot;\mathbb Z)` — and read off two contradictory facts at degree
`n = \dim X`:

- **Retraction ⇒ `\iota_*` injective.** From `f \circ \iota = \mathrm{id}_X`, functoriality gives
  `f_* \circ \iota_* = \mathrm{id}_{H_*(X)}`, so `\iota_*` has a left inverse and is injective.
- **Null-homotopy ⇒ `\iota_*` zero (in positive degrees).** A constant map factors through a point, and
  `H_k(\text{pt}) = 0` for `k \ge 1`, so a map homotopic to a constant induces the zero map on `H_k` for `k \ge 1`.
- **`X` has nonzero top homology.** A closed oriented `n`-manifold has `H_n(X;\mathbb Z) \ne 0` (the
  fundamental class `[X]` generates a `\mathbb Z`).

At degree `n`, `\iota_* : H_n(X) \to H_n(Y)` would have to be simultaneously injective and zero on a
nonzero group — impossible. Hence `\iota` is not homotopic to a constant.

## Complete Proof

Let `n = \dim X` and write `\iota : X \hookrightarrow Y` for the inclusion. The hypothesis `f|_X = \mathrm{id}_X`
means `f \circ \iota = \mathrm{id}_X`, so `f` is a retraction.

**Step 1 — `\iota_*` is injective on homology.** Applying the (covariant, homotopy-invariant) functor
`H_n(\cdot;\mathbb Z)` to `f \circ \iota = \mathrm{id}_X`,

$$f_* \circ \iota_* = (f \circ \iota)_* = (\mathrm{id}_X)_* = \mathrm{id}_{H_n(X)}.$$

Thus `\iota_* : H_n(X;\mathbb Z) \to H_n(Y;\mathbb Z)` admits the left inverse `f_*`, so `\iota_*` is
**injective**.

**Step 2 — `X` has nonzero top homology.** `X` is a compact (closed) oriented `n`-manifold without
boundary, so it possesses a fundamental class and `H_n(X;\mathbb Z) \ne 0`. (For `X` connected,
`H_n(X;\mathbb Z) \cong \mathbb Z`, generated by `[X]`; in general it is a nonzero free group, one `\mathbb Z`
per component.) In particular `n = \dim X \ge 1` and `H_n(X) \ne 0`.

**Step 3 — Suppose `\iota` were null-homotopic.** If `\iota` is homotopic to a constant map `c : X \to Y`,
then by homotopy invariance `\iota_* = c_*` on `H_n`. A constant map factors as `X \to \{p\} \hookrightarrow Y`,
so `c_*` factors through `H_n(\text{pt}) = 0` (as `n \ge 1`); hence `c_* = 0`, and therefore `\iota_* = 0`
on `H_n(X)`.

**Step 4 — Contradiction.** By Steps 1 and 3, `\iota_* : H_n(X) \to H_n(Y)` is both injective and the zero
map, while by Step 2 its domain `H_n(X) \ne 0`. An injective map out of a nonzero group cannot be zero —
contradiction.

Therefore `\iota : X \to Y` is **not** homotopic to a constant map. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Remark (the smooth/de Rham version).** The hypotheses are phrased in the smooth, oriented category, and
there is a parallel one-line proof using differential forms that uses orientation directly. Choose a volume
form `\omega` on `X` with `\int_X \omega \ne 0`, so `[\omega] \ne 0` in `H^n_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)`. Pull back by
the smooth retraction: `\iota^*(f^*\omega) = (f \circ \iota)^*\omega = \omega`, so
`\iota^*[f^*\omega] = [\omega] \ne 0`, i.e. `\iota^* : H^n_{\mathrm{dR}}(Y) \to H^n_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)` is
*surjective onto a nonzero class*. But if `\iota` were null-homotopic, `\iota^*` would be the zero map on
`H^n_{\mathrm{dR}}` for `n \ge 1` — contradiction. This is the cohomological mirror of the homology argument:
the retraction makes the induced map split (here surjective), the orientation supplies the nonzero top
class, and a null-homotopy would annihilate it. The result generalizes the classical "no retraction of the
disk onto its boundary sphere" and is the obstruction-theoretic heart of Brouwer-type fixed-point theorems.
