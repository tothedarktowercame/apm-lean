# APM b93J04: The splitting field of x⁴−10x²+1 is ℚ(√2,√3), with Galois group the Klein four-group

## Why It's Hard

The whole problem turns on one act of recognition: the messy nested radicals
$\sqrt{5\pm 2\sqrt6}$ are secretly $\sqrt2\pm\sqrt3$. Miss that and the field looks like
a degree-$4$ extension generated by an opaque root; see it and $K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt
2,\sqrt3)$, the cleanest biquadratic field, whose Galois group is the smallest
non-cyclic group $V_4$. Everything the problem asks — basis, group, *all* subfields —
then follows mechanically, but only after the simplification. (This polynomial is also
the textbook example of an irreducible quartic over $\mathbb{Q}$ that factors modulo
*every* prime, precisely because its group $V_4$ has no $4$-cycle.)

## The Key Insight

$x^4-10x^2+1=0\Rightarrow x^2=5\pm2\sqrt6$, and
$$
5+2\sqrt6=(\sqrt2+\sqrt3)^2,\qquad 5-2\sqrt6=(\sqrt3-\sqrt2)^2 .
$$
So the four roots are $\pm\sqrt2\pm\sqrt3$, and $K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2+\sqrt3)=\mathbb{Q}
(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$, with $[K:\mathbb{Q}]=4$. The Galois group is
$\{\sqrt2\mapsto\pm\sqrt2\}\times\{\sqrt3\mapsto\pm\sqrt3\}\cong (\mathbb{Z}/2)^2=V_4$,
acting on the four roots as the three double transpositions. Its three order-$2$
subgroups give exactly three quadratic subfields $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2),\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt3),
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt6)$.

## Complete Proof

### Finding the roots

Treating $x^4-10x^2+1$ as a quadratic in $x^2$,
$$
x^2=\frac{10\pm\sqrt{100-4}}{2}=5\pm\sqrt{24}=5\pm2\sqrt6 .
$$
Since $(\sqrt2+\sqrt3)^2=5+2\sqrt6$ and $(\sqrt2-\sqrt3)^2=5-2\sqrt6$, the four roots are
$$
\sqrt2+\sqrt3,\quad \sqrt2-\sqrt3,\quad -\sqrt2+\sqrt3,\quad -\sqrt2-\sqrt3
\ =\ \pm\sqrt2\pm\sqrt3 .
$$
Hence the splitting field is
$$
K=\mathbb{Q}(\pm\sqrt2\pm\sqrt3)=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2,\sqrt3),
$$
because the roots generate $\sqrt2=\tfrac12\big((\sqrt2+\sqrt3)+(\sqrt2-\sqrt3)\big)$ and
$\sqrt3$ likewise, and conversely $\sqrt2,\sqrt3$ generate all four roots.

### (a) A basis for $K$ over $\mathbb{Q}$

$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2)$ has $\mathbb{Q}$-basis $\{1,\sqrt2\}$ ($x^2-2$ irreducible).
Adjoining $\sqrt3$: $x^2-3$ has no root in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2)$ (if $\sqrt3=a+b\sqrt2$
then $3=a^2+2b^2+2ab\sqrt2$ forces $ab=0$, and neither $a^2=3$ nor $2b^2=3$ has a
rational solution), so $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2,\sqrt3):\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2)]=2$ and
$[K:\mathbb{Q}]=4$. A basis is
$$
\boxed{\{\,1,\ \sqrt2,\ \sqrt3,\ \sqrt6\,\}},\qquad \sqrt6=\sqrt2\,\sqrt3 .
$$
(In particular $x^4-10x^2+1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$: it is the minimal
polynomial of $\sqrt2+\sqrt3$, whose degree is $[K:\mathbb{Q}]=4$.)

### (b) The Galois group

$K/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois (splitting field of a separable polynomial in characteristic
$0$), with $|{\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})}|=[K:\mathbb{Q}]=4$. An automorphism is
determined by its effect on $\sqrt2$ and $\sqrt3$, each of which must go to a root of
its minimal polynomial, i.e. $\sqrt2\mapsto\pm\sqrt2$ and $\sqrt3\mapsto\pm\sqrt3$
independently. This gives four automorphisms:
$$
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 & \sqrt2 & \sqrt3 \\\hline
\mathrm{id} & \sqrt2 & \sqrt3\\
\sigma & -\sqrt2 & \sqrt3\\
\tau & \sqrt2 & -\sqrt3\\
\sigma\tau & -\sqrt2 & -\sqrt3
\end{array}
$$
(with $\sqrt6\mapsto(\pm)(\pm)\sqrt6$ accordingly). Each of $\sigma,\tau,\sigma\tau$ has
order $2$ and they commute, so
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})=\{\mathrm{id},\sigma,\tau,\sigma\tau\}\cong
\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2=V_4,
$$
the Klein four-group.

**Action on $K$ / on the roots.** Label the roots $r_1=\sqrt2+\sqrt3$,
$r_2=\sqrt2-\sqrt3$, $r_3=-\sqrt2+\sqrt3$, $r_4=-\sqrt2-\sqrt3$. Then:
$$
\sigma=(r_1\,r_3)(r_2\,r_4),\qquad \tau=(r_1\,r_2)(r_3\,r_4),\qquad
\sigma\tau=(r_1\,r_4)(r_2\,r_3).
$$
Thus $G$ is the Klein four subgroup of $S_4$ consisting of the identity and the three
**double transpositions** (it sits inside $A_4$); concretely, $\sigma$ flips the sign of
$\sqrt2$, $\tau$ flips the sign of $\sqrt3$, $\sigma\tau$ flips both.

### (c) All subfields of $K$

By the Galois correspondence, subfields $\mathbb{Q}\subseteq L\subseteq K$ correspond
to subgroups of $V_4$, of which there are exactly five: $\{\mathrm{id}\}$, the three
order-$2$ subgroups $\langle\sigma\rangle,\langle\tau\rangle,\langle\sigma\tau\rangle$,
and $V_4$. Computing fixed fields:

| subgroup | fixed field $L=K^{H}$ | $[L:\mathbb{Q}]$ |
|---|---|---|
| $V_4$ | $\mathbb{Q}$ | $1$ |
| $\langle\sigma\rangle$ ($\sqrt2\mapsto-\sqrt2$) | $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt3)$ | $2$ |
| $\langle\tau\rangle$ ($\sqrt3\mapsto-\sqrt3$) | $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2)$ | $2$ |
| $\langle\sigma\tau\rangle$ ($\sqrt2,\sqrt3\mapsto-\sqrt2,-\sqrt3$) | $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt6)$ | $2$ |
| $\{\mathrm{id}\}$ | $K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$ | $4$ |

(For $\langle\sigma\tau\rangle$: $\sigma\tau$ fixes $\sqrt6=\sqrt2\sqrt3\mapsto(-\sqrt2)
(-\sqrt3)=\sqrt6$ but negates $\sqrt2,\sqrt3$, so its fixed field is
$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt6)$.) Hence the subfields of $K$ are **exactly**
$$
\mathbb{Q},\quad \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2),\quad \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt3),\quad \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt6),
\quad K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2,\sqrt3),
$$
and there are no others. $\quad\blacksquare$

### Remark

All subgroups of $V_4$ are normal (it is abelian), so every subfield is Galois over
$\mathbb{Q}$ — consistent with the three quadratic subfields each being a splitting
field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt d)$. The absence of a $4$-cycle in $V_4\subset S_4$ is exactly
why $x^4-10x^2+1$, though irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, has no inert prime: its
factorization type mod $p$ is governed by cycle types in $V_4$, which are only $1+1+1+1$
or $2+2$ — never a single $4$-cycle — so it always factors modulo $p$.
