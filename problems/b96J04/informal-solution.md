# APM b96J04: The splitting field of x^8+1 — cyclotomic over ℚ, Frobenius over 𝔽₄₁

## Why It's Hard

The whole problem hinges on a single recognition: $x^8+1=\Phi_{16}(x)$, the
$16$th cyclotomic polynomial, so its roots are the **primitive $16$th roots of
unity**. Miss that and every part is a slog; see it and all three parts are
applications of standard cyclotomic/finite-field machinery. The subtleties are
local. In (a) you must *justify irreducibility* (it is $\Phi_{16}$, hence degree
$\varphi(16)=8$) and identify $(\mathbb{Z}/16)^\times$ correctly — it is **not
cyclic**: it is $\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/4$, a fact people misremember. In
(b) the Galois correspondence inverts everything: degree-$4$ *subfields*
correspond to *index-4* subgroups, i.e. subgroups of *order 2*, and "how many are
Galois" tests whether you know that **abelian** Galois group $\Rightarrow$ every
subgroup normal $\Rightarrow$ every subfield Galois. In (c) the field changes to
$\mathbb{F}_{41}$, where the Galois group is automatically **cyclic, generated by
Frobenius**, and the only real work is computing the degree $=\operatorname{ord}_{16}(41)$
— a small order computation, $41\equiv 9\pmod{16}$ and $9^2\equiv1$, giving $2$.

## The Key Insight

$x^8+1=\Phi_{16}(x)$ because $x^{16}-1=\prod_{d\mid16}\Phi_d(x)$ and
$x^8+1=\dfrac{x^{16}-1}{x^8-1}$ collects exactly the primitive $16$th roots. Then:

- **(a)** $K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{16})$ is cyclotomic, so
  $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/16)^\times$ via
  $\sigma_a(\zeta)=\zeta^a$, and $(\mathbb{Z}/16)^\times\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\times
  \mathbb{Z}/4$ (order $\varphi(16)=8$), generated by $\sigma_{-1}$ (order $2$)
  and $\sigma_5$ (order $4$).
- **(b)** Subfields of degree $4$ $\leftrightarrow$ subgroups of index $4$
  $=$ order $2$. In $\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/4$ the elements of order $2$ are
  $\{(1,0),(0,2),(1,2)\}$ — **three** of them, so **three** degree-$4$ subfields;
  $G$ abelian makes all subgroups normal, so **all three are Galois**.
- **(c)** Over $\mathbb{F}_{41}$, $L=\mathbb{F}_{41^d}$ with
  $\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{F}_{41})\cong\mathbb{Z}/d$ cyclic (Frobenius),
  where $d$ is least with $16\mid 41^d-1$, i.e.
  $d=\operatorname{ord}_{16}(41)=\operatorname{ord}_{16}(9)=2$. So the group is
  $\mathbb{Z}/2$.

## Complete Proof

**The roots.** Since $x^{16}-1=(x^8-1)(x^8+1)$, a root $\eta$ of $f(x)=x^8+1$
satisfies $\eta^{16}=1$ but $\eta^8=-1\neq1$, so $\eta$ has multiplicative order
exactly $16$: the roots of $f$ are precisely the **primitive $16$th roots of
unity**. Equivalently $f=\Phi_{16}$, the $16$th cyclotomic polynomial (degree
$\varphi(16)=8$).

### Part (a): $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/4$

Let $\zeta=\zeta_{16}=e^{2\pi i/16}$, a primitive $16$th root of unity; then
$K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$ is the splitting field (all roots are powers of $\zeta$).
The cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_{16}=x^8+1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, so
$$
[K:\mathbb{Q}]=\deg\Phi_{16}=\varphi(16)=8 .
$$
The extension is Galois (splitting field of a separable polynomial in
characteristic $0$), and each automorphism sends $\zeta$ to another primitive
$16$th root $\zeta^a$ with $\gcd(a,16)=1$; the map
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }(\mathbb{Z}/16)^\times,
\qquad \sigma_a(\zeta)=\zeta^a\ \mapsto\ a,
$$
is the standard group isomorphism (it is a homomorphism because
$\sigma_a\sigma_b(\zeta)=\zeta^{ab}$, injective since $\sigma_a$ is determined by
$a$, and both sides have order $8$).

Finally $(\mathbb{Z}/16)^\times=\{1,3,5,7,9,11,13,15\}$ is **not cyclic**: every
element squares into $\langle 5\rangle$... more simply, $-1\equiv15$ has order $2$
and $5$ has order $4$ ($5^2=25\equiv9,\ 5^4\equiv1$), with $\langle
5\rangle=\{1,5,9,13\}$ not containing $-1=15$; hence
$$
(\mathbb{Z}/16)^\times=\langle-1\rangle\times\langle5\rangle\cong
\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/4 .
$$
Therefore $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/4$
(abelian of order $8$, exponent $4$, not cyclic). $\quad\checkmark$

### Part (b): three degree-4 subfields, all three Galois

By the fundamental theorem of Galois theory, intermediate fields $M$ with
$[M:\mathbb{Q}]=4$ correspond bijectively to subgroups $H\le G$ with
$[G:H]=[M:\mathbb{Q}]=4$, i.e. $|H|=|G|/4=2$. So **degree-$4$ subfields $=$
order-$2$ subgroups of $G$**, and each order-$2$ subgroup is $\{1,\sigma\}$ for a
unique involution $\sigma$, so I count elements of order $2$.

In $G\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/4$ write elements $(a,b)$,
$a\in\mathbb{Z}/2$, $b\in\mathbb{Z}/4$. Then $2(a,b)=(0,2b)=(0,0)$ iff
$b\in\{0,2\}$, so the elements of order dividing $2$ are
$(0,0),(1,0),(0,2),(1,2)$ — the $2$-torsion is a Klein four-group — and exactly
$3$ of them are nontrivial. (Concretely in $(\mathbb{Z}/16)^\times$ these are the
$a$ with $a^2\equiv1\pmod{16}$, namely $a=7,9,15$.) Hence there are exactly
$$
\boxed{3\text{ subfields of degree }4.}
$$

Since $G$ is **abelian**, every subgroup $H$ is normal in $G$, and by the Galois
correspondence the corresponding subfield $M=K^H$ is then **Galois over
$\mathbb{Q}$** (normal subgroup $\iff$ normal subextension). Therefore **all $3$
of these degree-$4$ subfields are Galois over $\mathbb{Q}$.** $\quad\checkmark$

*(Concretely, the three involutions $\sigma_{15},\sigma_9,\sigma_7$ give the
maximal real field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})=\mathbb{Q}(\cos\frac\pi8)$ (fixed
by complex conjugation $\sigma_{-1}$), the field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta^2)=\mathbb{Q}
(\zeta_8)=\mathbb{Q}(i,\sqrt2)$ (fixed by $\sigma_9:\zeta\mapsto\zeta^9=-\zeta$),
and $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta-\zeta^{-1})$ (fixed by $\sigma_7:\zeta\mapsto-\zeta^{-1}$);
each is degree $4$ and Galois, with order-$4$ abelian Galois group $G/H$.)*

### Part (c): $\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{F}_{41})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2$

First, $f=x^8+1$ is **separable** over $\mathbb{F}_{41}$: its derivative $8x^7$ is
nonzero ($41\nmid8$) and shares no root with $f$ (the only root of $8x^7$ is $0$,
not a root of $f$). So $f$ has $8$ distinct roots in $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{41}$,
again the elements of multiplicative order exactly $16$ (valid because
$\gcd(16,41)=1$).

The splitting field of any polynomial over $\mathbb{F}_{41}$ is a finite field
$L=\mathbb{F}_{41^d}$, and finite-field extensions are Galois with **cyclic**
group generated by the Frobenius $\operatorname{Frob}(x)=x^{41}$:
$\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{41^d}/\mathbb{F}_{41})\cong\mathbb{Z}/d$. It
remains to find $d=[L:\mathbb{F}_{41}]$.

$L$ is the smallest extension of $\mathbb{F}_{41}$ containing a primitive $16$th
root of unity. The group $\mathbb{F}_{41^d}^\times$ is cyclic of order $41^d-1$,
and it contains an element of order $16$ iff
$$
16\mid 41^d-1 .
$$
Thus $d$ is the least positive integer with $41^d\equiv1\pmod{16}$, i.e.
$d=\operatorname{ord}_{16}(41)$. Now $41\equiv9\pmod{16}$ and
$$
9^1\equiv9,\qquad 9^2=81\equiv1\pmod{16},
$$
so $\operatorname{ord}_{16}(41)=2$. (Check: $41-1=40$ is not divisible by $16$, so
$d\neq1$ — indeed $f$ has no root in $\mathbb{F}_{41}$ — while
$41^2-1=1680=16\cdot105$.) Hence $d=2$,
$$
L=\mathbb{F}_{41^2}=\mathbb{F}_{1681},\qquad
\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{F}_{41})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2.}
$$
$\blacksquare$

### Remark

Everything follows from "$x^8+1=\Phi_{16}$." Over $\mathbb{Q}$ the Galois group is
the full unit group $(\mathbb{Z}/16)^\times\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/4$;
its lattice of three order-$2$ subgroups gives three degree-$4$ subfields, all
Galois by abelianness. Over $\mathbb{F}_{41}$ the group collapses to the cyclic
image of Frobenius, whose order is the order of $41$ in $(\mathbb{Z}/16)^\times$,
namely $2$ — so $\mathbb{F}_{41}$ "sees" only the order-$2$ subgroup generated by
$\sigma_{41}=\sigma_9$, and correspondingly $x^8+1$ factors into four irreducible
**quadratics** over $\mathbb{F}_{41}$ (each root lies in $\mathbb{F}_{41^2}$ but
not $\mathbb{F}_{41}$). The same Galois-group-as-subgroup-of-$(\mathbb{Z}/n)^\times$
mechanism governs both the characteristic-$0$ and finite-field pictures; only the
*image* changes.
