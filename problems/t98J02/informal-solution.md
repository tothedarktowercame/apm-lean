# Informal solution

An embedded circle in a closed surface is separating exactly when its class
vanishes in \(H_1(-;\mathbb Z/2)\); these coefficients also cover the case
where the surface is nonorientable.

For (a), if \(\widetilde C\) separated, then
\([\widetilde C]=0\in H_1(\widetilde F;\mathbb Z/2)\). Naturality gives
\(p_*[\widetilde C]=[C]\), because the restricted covering has degree one.
Thus \([C]=0\), contradicting that (C) is nonseparating. Hence (a) is true.

Statement (b) is false. Take a genus-two surface split by a separating curve
\(C\) into two once-punctured tori (A) and (B). Choose a connected double
cover whose restrictions over both (A) and (B) are connected (equivalently,
choose a homomorphism \(\pi_1(F)\to\mathbb Z/2\) nonzero on a loop in each
side). Since the boundary word (C) maps to zero, its inverse image consists
of two circles, each mapping homeomorphically to (C). The lifted surface is
obtained by joining the connected lifted (A) and (B) by these two boundary
circles. Cutting along just one of them leaves the other joining the two
pieces, so that lift is nonseparating although (C) is separating.
