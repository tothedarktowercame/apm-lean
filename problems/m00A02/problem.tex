Let $f$ be a real-valued function of a real variable.
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{alist}
%------------------------------------------------------------------*
\item Define what it means for $f$ to be asymptotic to the series
$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_n}{x^n}$ as $x\rightarrow +\infty$.
%------------------------------------------------------------------*
\item Show that $f(x)=e^{-x}$ is asymptotic to the null expansion
where $a_n=0$ for all $n$.
%------------------------------------------------------------------*
\item Show by example that even if $f$ is infinitely differentiable,
formal differentiation of its asymptotic expansion need not lead to a
valid asymptotic expansion. That is, it is not necessarily true that
$f(x)\sim \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_n}{x^n}$ as $x\rightarrow \infty$
implies $f'(x)\sim \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\frac{a_n}{x^n}$ as
$x\rightarrow \infty$.
%------------------------------------------------------------------*
\item Give hypotheses under which the implication \emph{does} hold and
prove your result.
%------------------------------------------------------------------*
\end{alist}
