# APM t01A04: π₁ of a torus wedge a circle, and two non-isomorphic double covers

## Why It's Hard

The space `X = T^2 \vee S^1` (a torus and a circle joined at one point `x`) has a fundamental group that is a
*free product* `\pi_1(T^2) * \pi_1(S^1)` — the wedge introduces no relations between the two pieces, so `c`
(the extra circle) does **not** commute with the torus generators `a, b`, even though `a, b` commute with
each other. Keeping straight which generators commute and which are free is the first subtlety.

Part (b) is where the geometry bites. Index-`2` subgroups correspond to surjections `\pi_1(X) \to \mathbb Z/2`,
and there are several; the task is to pick *two* whose covering spaces are genuinely non-isomorphic and to
*see* the difference. The crucial realization is that a double cover is built by choosing **which generators
to unwrap**, and unwrapping the free circle `c` produces a completely different shape from unwrapping a torus
generator `a`: the former yields two tori bridged by a circle, the latter yields a single (doubled) torus
with two circles attached. Telling them apart cleanly — they have the same Euler characteristic — requires a
finer invariant like `H_2` (number of independent torus `2`-cycles).

## The Key Insight

**(a)** Van Kampen for a wedge gives a free product:

$$\pi_1(X, x) = \pi_1(T^2) * \pi_1(S^1) = \langle a, b \mid [a,b] \rangle * \langle c \rangle
= \langle a, b, c \mid aba^{-1}b^{-1} = 1\rangle.$$

`a, b` are the two torus loops (meridian/longitude), `c` the extra circle; `a, b` commute, `c` is free.

**(b)** Index-`2` subgroups are kernels of surjections `\varphi : \pi_1(X) \to \mathbb Z/2`, determined by
`(\varphi(a), \varphi(b), \varphi(c)) \in (\mathbb Z/2)^3 \setminus 0` (the relation `[a,b]` is automatic in
abelian `\mathbb Z/2`). Two choices give non-isomorphic covers:

- `\varphi_1 : a, b \mapsto 0,\ c \mapsto 1` — unwrap **the wedge circle**. Cover: **two** copies of `T^2`,
  joined by a single circle (the connected double cover of `S^1`). Here `H_2 = \mathbb Z^2`.
- `\varphi_2 : a \mapsto 1,\ b, c \mapsto 0` — unwrap **a torus generator**. Cover: **one** torus (the
  connected double cover of `T^2`) with **two** circles attached. Here `H_2 = \mathbb Z`.

Since `H_2(\text{cover}_1) = \mathbb Z^2 \ne \mathbb Z = H_2(\text{cover}_2)`, the covers are non-isomorphic.

## Complete Proof

### (a) Presentation of `\pi_1(X, x)`

Write `X = T^2 \vee S^1`, wedged at `x`. Cover `X` by open sets `A \supset T^2` and `B \supset S^1`, each
deformation retracting to its piece, with `A \cap B` a contractible neighborhood of `x` (so
`\pi_1(A \cap B) = 1`). By the **van Kampen theorem**,

$$\pi_1(X, x) \cong \pi_1(T^2) * \pi_1(S^1).$$

Now `\pi_1(T^2) = \langle a, b \mid aba^{-1}b^{-1}\rangle \cong \mathbb Z^2` and `\pi_1(S^1) = \langle c\rangle \cong \mathbb Z`, so

$$\boxed{\ \pi_1(X, x) = \langle a, b, c \mid aba^{-1}b^{-1} = 1\rangle \cong \mathbb Z^2 * \mathbb Z.\ }$$

**Picture / generators.** In `X`, the loop `a` runs once around the *meridian* of the torus, `b` once around
its *longitude* (these commute — slide one around the other on the torus surface), and `c` runs once around
the extra circle glued on at `x`. The single relation `[a,b] = 1` is the torus; `c` satisfies no relation
(it is free), in particular it does not commute with `a` or `b`.

### (b) Two index-`2` subgroups with non-isomorphic covers

Index-`2` subgroups are normal and correspond bijectively to **surjections** `\varphi : \pi_1(X) \to \mathbb Z/2`
via their kernels. Such a `\varphi` is determined by `(\varphi(a), \varphi(b), \varphi(c)) \in (\mathbb Z/2)^3`
(the relation `[a,b] \mapsto 0` holds automatically since `\mathbb Z/2` is abelian), and must be nonzero to be
onto. We choose two:

**Subgroup `H_1 = \ker\varphi_1`,** `\varphi_1(a) = \varphi_1(b) = 0`, `\varphi_1(c) = 1`:
`H_1` consists of all words with **even total `c`-exponent**; as a subgroup it is generated by `a, b, c^2` and
the conjugates `c a c^{-1}, c b c^{-1}` (index `2`).

*Covering space (sketch).* Since `\varphi_1(a) = \varphi_1(b) = 0`, the loops `a, b` lift to loops, so each
of the two sheets over `x` carries a full torus. Since `\varphi_1(c) = 1`, the circle `c` lifts to a *path*
joining the two basepoints `x_0, x_1`, and its double cover is one connected circle through both. So:

```
     T²(x₀) ───●── E ──●─── T²(x₁)
```

two copies of `T^2` (at `x_0` and `x_1`) bridged by a single circle `E` (the connected double cover of the
wedge circle), passing through one point of each torus. It has `H_2 = \mathbb Z^2` (two independent torus
`2`-cycles).

**Subgroup `H_2 = \ker\varphi_2`,** `\varphi_2(a) = 1`, `\varphi_2(b) = \varphi_2(c) = 0`:
`H_2` consists of all words with **even total `a`-exponent**; it is generated by `a^2, b, c` and the
conjugates `a b a^{-1}, a c a^{-1}` (index `2`).

*Covering space (sketch).* Since `\varphi_2(a) = 1`, `\varphi_2(b) = 0`, the torus is *connectedly*
double-covered in the `a`-direction — its double cover `\widetilde{T^2}` is again a single torus (unwrapping
one `S^1`-factor twofold gives `S^1 \times S^1`), with two preimages `x_0, x_1` of `x`. Since
`\varphi_2(c) = 0`, the circle `c` lifts to a *loop* at **each** of `x_0, x_1`. So:

```
        C₀          C₁
        ●           ●
         \         /
          ╲       ╱
           [  T̃²  ]      (one torus, two circles C₀, C₁ wedged on)
```

a single (doubled) torus with two circles `C_0, C_1` attached at the two basepoints. It has `H_2 = \mathbb Z`
(one torus `2`-cycle).

**Non-isomorphism.** `H_1 \ne H_2` (they differ on `a` and `c`), and for index-`2` (normal) subgroups,
covers are isomorphic iff the subgroups are equal; so the covers are non-isomorphic. More vividly, the total
spaces are not even homotopy equivalent:

$$H_2(\text{cover}_1) = \mathbb Z^2 \ne \mathbb Z = H_2(\text{cover}_2).$$

$$\boxed{\ H_1 = \ker(\varphi_1: c \mapsto 1,\ a,b \mapsto 0)\ \text{ and }\ H_2 = \ker(\varphi_2: a \mapsto 1,\ b,c \mapsto 0)\ }$$

are two index-`2` subgroups giving non-isomorphic covering spaces. ∎

**Remark.** Both covers have the same Euler characteristic (`\chi = 2\chi(X) = 2(0 + 0 - 1) = -2`), so `\chi`
cannot distinguish them — the right invariant is `H_2`, which counts torus `2`-cycles. The geometric moral is
that *which generator you unwrap* controls the shape of the double cover: unwrapping the **free** wedge circle
`c` keeps each torus intact but produces **two** of them (bridged by the doubled circle), while unwrapping a
**torus** generator `a` merges the sheets into **one** larger torus (but doubles the count of attached
circles). The free product structure of `\pi_1`, with `c` non-commuting and `a, b` commuting, is exactly what
makes these two behaviors available.
