# Blueprint for t99A01

## Triage and repair

Verdict: **unfaithful; repaired**.

The registered theorem is true algebra, but it does not state the problem in
`problem.md`.  The source asks for the fundamental group and **all homology
groups of the spaces** obtained by attaching disks to a circle.  The old Lean
theorem mentions none of `X`, `Y`, or `Z`; it asserts calculations for three
presented groups and three kernels.  In particular it omits `H₀`, the
cokernel calculations giving `H₁`, and all higher-degree vanishing.  This is
the defect identified by `holes/labs/statement-registry/triage-3.md`.

There is consequently no `Refute.lean`: the old proposition is not false and
has no counterexample; it is a stand-in for only part of the requested result.

`RevisedMain.lean` makes the smallest semantic repair recommended by the
grader.  For a degree vector `degree : Fin r → ℕ`, it defines the actual space
as the quotient of

`Circle ⊕ (Fin r × closedBall (0 : ℂ) 1)`

by the equivalence relation generated by identifying the boundary point `z`
of disk `i` with `z ^ degree i` on the common circle.  The topology is the
quotient topology.  The vectors `(2)`, `(2,4)`, and `(2,4,5)` define `X`, `Y`,
and `Z`.  The revised conclusion states their based fundamental groups, their
integral singular homology in degrees zero through two, and vanishing in every
degree at least three.  Thus every item requested by `problem.md` is present.
No hypothesis was added.

The grader's objection is eliminated rather than merely excluded by a new
hypothesis: every conclusion now refers to an explicit quotient space.

### Truth checks

I checked the cellular calculation by hand.  Each quotient has one vertex,
one oriented one-cell, and respectively one, two, or three two-cells.  Thus
`∂₁ = 0`, while `∂₂` is `(2)`, `(2 4)`, or `(2 4 5)`.  This gives

* `X`: `H₀ = ℤ`, `H₁ = ℤ/2`, `H₂ = 0`;
* `Y`: `H₀ = ℤ`, `H₁ = ℤ/2`, `H₂ = ℤ`, with kernel generated by `(-2,1)`;
* `Z`: `H₀ = ℤ`, `H₁ = 0`, `H₂ = ℤ²` (the coefficients have gcd one).

There are no cells above dimension two.  Van Kampen gives the presentations
`⟨a | a²⟩`, `⟨a | a²,a⁴⟩`, and `⟨a | a²,a⁴,a⁵⟩`, hence `ℤ/2`, `ℤ/2`, and the
trivial group.  As degeneracy checks, the disk index types have cardinalities
exactly one, two, and three (none is empty), every degree is positive, and the
common circle and chosen basepoint are explicit.  The boundary endpoint has
norm exactly one and `circleInDisk` proves it lies in the closed disk.  The
third presentation and `H₁(Z)` both become trivial because `gcd(2,4,5)=1`,
not because the quotient or an index type degenerates.

## Independent leaf lemmas

All fifteen leaves below are top-level and mutually independent: none uses
another leaf as a hypothesis or theorem.  This is important because seats can
land in any order.  The estimates are for the specialization once the named
library boundary exists.  At present, the three fundamental-group leaves need
the missing two-cell van Kampen specialization, and the twelve homology leaves
need the missing finite-CW cellular/singular comparison.  Those foundations
are genuinely larger than a seat and are not hidden in an overloaded helper.

### Fundamental group leaves

* `t99A01_piOne_X` (40–80 lines): apply two-cell van Kampen to the degree-two
  attaching map, then identify `⟨a | a^2⟩` with `Multiplicative (ZMod 2)`.
  Useful existing API: `FundamentalGroup.map`, `FundamentalGroup.mapOfEq`,
  `FundamentalGroup.fundamentalGroupMulEquivOfPathConnected`; missing API:
  quotient by the normal closure of an attached loop.
* `t99A01_piOne_Y` (50–100 lines): the same construction for relators
  `a^2,a^4`; simplify the normal closure using divisibility (`2 ∣ 4`).
* `t99A01_piOne_Z` (50–100 lines): use relators `a^2,a^4,a^5`; Bézout
  (`5 - 2*2 = 1`) kills the generator, giving `Subsingleton`.

The quotient has a nonempty circle summand in every case and the basepoint is
the image of `1 : Circle`.  Thus none of these claims is vacuous.  The degrees
are positive, so no constant attaching map edge case occurs.

### Low-degree homology leaves

* `t99A01_hZero_X`, `t99A01_hZero_Y`, `t99A01_hZero_Z` (30–60 lines each):
  connected finite-CW `H₀`; use the cellular/singular comparison and the
  one-vertex cellular boundary `∂₁ = 0`.
* `t99A01_hOne_X` (40–80 lines): cokernel of multiplication by `2`; use
  `LinearMap.quotKerEquivRange`/quotient-module isomorphism tools and the
  standard `Int` to `ZMod 2` quotient map.
* `t99A01_hTwo_X` (30–60 lines): kernel of multiplication by `2` is zero;
  use `Int.mul_eq_zero` and `Limits.isZero_of_iso_of_zero` after comparison.
* `t99A01_hOne_Y` (50–90 lines): cokernel of the row `(2,4)` is `ZMod 2`;
  reduce its image to `2ℤ` using `dvd_add` and the first basis vector.
* `t99A01_hTwo_Y` (60–110 lines): kernel of `(x,y) ↦ 2*x+4*y` is freely
  generated by `(-2,1)`; build the explicit linear equivalence and use
  `LinearEquiv.ofBijective`.
* `t99A01_hOne_Z` (50–90 lines): `(2,4,5)` is surjective since
  `2*(-2)+5*1=1`; construct a preimage for every integer and identify the
  cokernel as zero.
* `t99A01_hTwo_Z` (80–140 lines): the kernel of
  `(x,y,z) ↦ 2*x+4*y+5*z` is free on `(-2,1,0)` and `(-5,0,2)` (or use a
  unimodular change of basis); package with `LinearEquiv.ofBijective`.

These statements concern the concrete nonempty index types `Fin 1`, `Fin 2`,
and `Fin 3`.  The `Z` rank-two claim uses a primitive row (gcd one), not merely
a rank count; this handles the torsion pitfall.  Boundary signs can negate a
row but do not change any kernel or cokernel isomorphism; a proof should fix
the circle orientation once and compute the displayed positive-degree row.

### Higher-degree leaves

* `t99A01_hHigher_X`, `t99A01_hHigher_Y`, `t99A01_hHigher_Z` (20–45 lines
  each): for arbitrary `k ≥ 3`, transport the zero cellular chain groups in
  degrees above two through the cellular/singular comparison.  Normalize
  inequalities with `omega`; discharge the categorical result with
  `Limits.IsZero` transport lemmas such as `Limits.isZero_of_iso_of_zero`.

The quantifier is not empty (`k = 3` is allowed), and the argument must cover
all larger natural degrees rather than just degree three.

### Missing shared library foundations

The closest repository work is
`ConstructionTargets.PointWedgeVanKampen`,
`ConstructionTargets.FundamentalGroupConcrete`,
`ConstructionTargets.SingularHomologyConcrete`, and
`ConstructionTargets.IntegralSingularHomologyComparison`.  Mathlib provides
the singular complex and homology functor but not a ready theorem computing
these explicit adjunction-space quotients by cellular chains.  A reusable
two-cell van Kampen theorem and a finite two-dimensional CW
cellular/singular comparison are each a **LIBRARY** project (roughly
1,500–4,000 lines together).  Until they exist, the leaf estimates above are
conditional; proving any topology leaf completely from current primitives is
not honestly a 30–150-line task.

## Assembly and dependency layers

* **Layer 1:** the fifteen independent leaves listed above.
* **Layer 2 (sorry-free):** `t99A01_attached_cells_computation` assembles the
  twelve fixed-degree conclusions and combines the three higher-degree leaves
  pointwise in `k`.
* **Layer 3 (already sorry-free):** `apm_t99a01` applies the assembly lemma.

The assembly carries no mathematics and no admission.  In particular, no
leaf's axiom set can acquire another leaf's `sorryAx` through a dependency.
