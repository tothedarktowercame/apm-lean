# Blueprint for t99A01

## Triage and repair

Verdict: **unfaithful; repaired**.

The registered theorem is true algebra, but it does not state the problem in
`problem.md`.  The source asks for the fundamental group and **all homology
groups of the spaces** obtained by attaching disks to a circle.  The old Lean
theorem mentions none of `X`, `Y`, or `Z`; it asserts calculations for three
presented groups and three kernels.  In particular it omits `H₀`, the
cokernel calculations giving `H₁`, and all higher-degree vanishing.  This is
the defect identified by `holes/labs/statement-registry/triage-3.md`.

There is consequently no `Refute.lean`: the old proposition is not false and
has no counterexample; it is a stand-in for only part of the requested result.

`RevisedMain.lean` makes the smallest semantic repair recommended by the
grader.  For a degree vector `degree : Fin r → ℕ`, it defines the actual space
as the quotient of

`Circle ⊕ (Fin r × closedBall (0 : ℂ) 1)`

by the equivalence relation generated by identifying the boundary point `z`
of disk `i` with `z ^ degree i` on the common circle.  The topology is the
quotient topology.  The vectors `(2)`, `(2,4)`, and `(2,4,5)` define `X`, `Y`,
and `Z`.  The revised conclusion states their based fundamental groups, their
integral singular homology in degrees zero through two, and vanishing in every
degree at least three.  Thus every item requested by `problem.md` is present.
No hypothesis was added.

The grader's objection is eliminated rather than merely excluded by a new
hypothesis: every conclusion now refers to an explicit quotient space.

### Truth checks

I checked the cellular calculation by hand.  Each quotient has one vertex,
one oriented one-cell, and respectively one, two, or three two-cells.  Thus
`∂₁ = 0`, while `∂₂` is `(2)`, `(2 4)`, or `(2 4 5)`.  This gives

* `X`: `H₀ = ℤ`, `H₁ = ℤ/2`, `H₂ = 0`;
* `Y`: `H₀ = ℤ`, `H₁ = ℤ/2`, `H₂ = ℤ`, with kernel generated by `(-2,1)`;
* `Z`: `H₀ = ℤ`, `H₁ = 0`, `H₂ = ℤ²` (the coefficients have gcd one).

There are no cells above dimension two.  Van Kampen gives the presentations
`⟨a | a²⟩`, `⟨a | a²,a⁴⟩`, and `⟨a | a²,a⁴,a⁵⟩`, hence `ℤ/2`, `ℤ/2`, and the
trivial group.  As degeneracy checks, the disk index types have cardinalities
exactly one, two, and three (none is empty), every degree is positive, and the
common circle and chosen basepoint are explicit.  The boundary endpoint has
norm exactly one and `circleInDisk` proves it lies in the closed disk.  The
third presentation and `H₁(Z)` both become trivial because `gcd(2,4,5)=1`,
not because the quotient or an index type degenerates.

## Remaining lemma

### `t99A01_attached_cells_computation` — LIBRARY

**Statement in words.** For the three explicit quotient spaces just defined,
compute the based fundamental groups as `ℤ/2`, `ℤ/2`, and trivial, and compute
all integral singular homology groups according to the table above, with
vanishing in degrees at least three.

**Why true.** Give each quotient its evident finite CW structure.  A van
Kampen theorem for adjoining two-cells identifies the fundamental group with
the quotient of the circle group by the normal closures of the degree loops.
The circle generator then gives the three elementary presentations above.
For homology, the cellular chain complex is

`0 → ℤ^r --(degree)→ ℤ --0→ ℤ → 0`.

Smith normal form (or direct Bézout calculations) gives its kernels and
cokernels.  A cellular/singular comparison transports this calculation to
Mathlib's `singularHomologyFunctor` objects.  The same comparison gives
vanishing above the CW dimension.

This is deliberately one library boundary.  The closest sorry-free repository
infrastructure is the fundamental-groupoid van Kampen program rooted at
`ConstructionTargets.PointWedgeVanKampen`, and the singular chain machinery
in `ConstructionTargets.SingularHomologyConcrete` and
`ConstructionTargets.IntegralSingularHomologyComparison`.  None currently
supplies the missing theorem for adjoining a two-cell or a general finite-CW
cellular/singular comparison.  `ConstructionTargets/FundamentalGroupConcrete.md`
explicitly lists specialization to an attached two-cell and quotient by the
normal closure as unfinished.  The required general comparison is well above
300 lines, so splitting its three consequences into seats would duplicate the
same topological construction and would not create independent work.

**Expected size.** A multi-file ConstructionTargets program, approximately
1,500–4,000 lines.  Once a general two-cell attachment API and cellular
comparison exist, this specialization should be under 150 lines.

**Dependencies.** None inside this blueprint.  Externally: two-cell van
Kampen, finite two-dimensional CW cellular/singular homology comparison, and
elementary Smith-normal-form specialization.

**Truth check.** The hand van Kampen and chain-complex computations above
check every component.  I also checked the potentially dangerous endpoints:
the use of a closed disk includes its boundary, all attachment degrees are
nonzero, and degree five makes the last boundary map surjective.  I have no
doubt about the mathematical statement; the uncertainty is solely the size
of the missing library infrastructure.
