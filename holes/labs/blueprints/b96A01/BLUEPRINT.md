## Triage and repair

**Verdict: the printed problem is false; the current Lean statement contains the necessary minimal repair.** Parts (a)–(c) are faithful. Part (d) says merely that `G` is finite simple, but the intended proof uses that `G` is nonabelian. Take `n = 1`, `G = {±1} ⊆ GL₁(ℚ)`, and `p = 2`. This `G` is simple of order two, while `GL₁(ℤ/2ℤ)` is trivial, so no injection exists.

The source itself is therefore defective. Its informal argument explicitly reaches a contradiction only from **nonabelian** simplicity. `RevisedMain.lean` records the smallest warranted repair: the hypothesis

```text
¬(∀ a b : G, a * b = b * a)
```

in part (d). This is exactly the repair already present in `problems/b96A01/lean/Main.lean`; no further alteration was made. Part (a) also correctly asks for some finite subset of some basis, rather than asserting that an arbitrary supplied generating finset is independent.

Degenerate checks: for `V = 0`, part (a) uses the empty basis subset; for the trivial subgroup in part (b), `P = 1` works; in part (c), `p = 0` or `p = 1` makes the relevant reduction kernel trivial, so allowing arbitrary natural `p` causes no counterexample. Part (d)'s explicit noncommutativity hypothesis excludes both the trivial group and all cyclic simple groups.

## Remaining lemmas

### `apm_b96a01_part_a`

**Statement.** Every finitely generated additive subgroup of a finite-dimensional rational vector space is generated by a finite subset of a vector-space basis.

**Proof sketch.** Regard `A` as a finitely generated torsion-free abelian group. Over the PID `ℤ`, it is free of finite rank. Choose a `ℤ`-basis of `A`; clearing denominators shows that its images in `V` are `ℚ`-linearly independent. Extend that independent family to a basis of `V`, and take the finite set of indices occupied by the original generators. Mathlib's PID/free-module basis infrastructure and basis extension machinery should handle the two structural steps; the main local work is relating the additive closure to integer spans.

**Estimate.** 100–200 lines.

**Dependencies.** None.

**Truth check.** For `V = ℚ` and `A = (1/d)ℤ`, the single vector `1/d` is a rational basis and generates `A`. For `A = 0`, the finite index set is empty. Dependent input generators such as `{1,2}` do not matter because the conclusion chooses a new generating set.

### `apm_b96a01_part_b`

**Statement.** Every finite subgroup of `GLₙ(ℚ)` is rationally conjugate to matrices with integral entries.

**Proof sketch.** Let `L` be the additive subgroup generated by all translates of the standard lattice `ℤⁿ` under `G`. Finiteness of `G` makes `L` finitely generated; it contains the standard lattice and hence spans `ℚⁿ`. Apply part (a) to obtain a `ℤ`-basis of `L` which is also a rational basis. Since `gL = L` (use `g⁻¹ ∈ G`), every group element has integral coordinates in this basis. The inverse of the corresponding basis matrix supplies the orientation `P * g * P⁻¹` used by the formal statement. Invertibility over `ℤ` is not required by the conclusion; integrality entrywise is sufficient.

**Estimate.** 180–300 lines.

**Dependencies.** Part (a).

**Truth check.** For `G = {±1} ⊆ GL₁(ℚ)`, the standard lattice is already stable and `P = 1`. Conjugating a rational order-two matrix by a lattice basis produces the familiar integral representation.

### `apm_b96a01_part_c`

**Statement.** For `m ≥ 2`, the kernel of reduction from `GLₙ(ZMod (p^m))` to `GLₙ(ZMod (p^(m-1)))` is abelian.

**Proof sketch.** Extensionality reduces equality of general-linear-group elements to matrix entries. A kernel element is congruent to the identity modulo `p^(m-1)`, so write it as `I + p^(m-1)A`. For two such matrices, the two cross-products differ only by terms multiplied by `p^(2m-2)`, which vanish modulo `p^m` because `2m-2 ≥ m`. Therefore both multiplication orders equal `I + p^(m-1)(A+B)`. The proof must package the lift/divisibility calculation for `ZMod`; no new conceptual theorem is needed.

**Estimate.** 100–200 lines.

**Dependencies.** None.

**Truth check.** For `n = 1` the whole ambient group is already abelian. At `m = 2`, the discarded product has coefficient `p²`, exactly zero modulo `p²`. For `p = 0` and `p = 1`, the reduction is respectively an identity-like or trivial-ring map and the kernel remains commutative.

### `apm_b96a01_part_d`

**Statement.** A finite nonabelian simple subgroup of `GLₙ(ℚ)` injects into `GLₙ(ZMod p)` for every prime `p`.

**Proof sketch.** First use part (b) and conjugation to replace `G` by an integral matrix group. Reduction modulo `p` gives a homomorphism whose kernel is normal, hence either bottom or top by simplicity. If it is top, place `G` successively in the congruence subgroups modulo `p^k`. At each stage part (c) makes the next quotient abelian; any map from nonabelian simple `G` to that quotient is trivial. Thus `G` lies in every congruence subgroup. An integer matrix congruent to the identity modulo every `p^k` is the identity, contradicting nonabelianness. Hence the original reduction has trivial kernel.

**Estimate.** 180–300 lines.

**Dependencies.** Parts (b) and (c).

**Truth check.** The excluded `C₂`, `p = 2`, `n = 1` example demonstrates why the repair is necessary. For a nonabelian simple group, any homomorphism to an abelian group is trivial because its kernel cannot be bottom (otherwise the group would be abelian). The intersection of the integral congruence filtration is visibly `{I}` entrywise.
