# Refutation of `apm_m95a03`

The final conjunct asserts uniqueness by literal equality of functions
`ℝ → ℝ`, but the energy and boundary constraints observe a function only on
`[a,b]`.  Thus any minimizer can be changed by a nonzero smooth bump supported
strictly outside `[a,b]`, producing a distinct global `C²` minimizer with the
same energy and endpoint values.

`Counterexample.lean` compiles a concrete bump for `[0,1]`, centered at `2`
with outer radius `1/2`.  It proves:

- the bump is `ContDiff ℝ 2`;
- it and its derivative vanish at every point of `[0,1]`;
- its value at `2` is `1`, so it is not the zero function.

Adding this bump to either purported unique minimizer leaves every integrand
and both endpoint conditions unchanged, contradicting global uniqueness.
The statement would become viable with uniqueness modulo `Set.EqOn (Icc a b)`
or with functions defined on the interval subtype.
