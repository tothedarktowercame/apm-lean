### m93J02

### m02J02

The source asks simply “Find a basis” (`problem.tex:1-3`) in the interval, half-line, and line cases (`problem.tex:7-11`). Lean's custom `IsH10Basis` is introduced at `Main.lean:45` and the final theorem supplies sine, odd Hermite, and full Hermite families (`Main.lean:7107` onward). ### m00A04

The original is the standard Riesz/minimization proof of the weak Dirichlet problem (`problem.tex:1-19`) and is sound (the apparent `V≠u` at line 10 is plainly the typo `v≠u`). Lean defines test functions using `IsCompact (support φ)` (`Main.lean:28-31`), not compact closure. For a continuous nonzero function on positive-dimensional Euclidean space, raw support is open around a nonzero point and cannot be compact; the file consequently proves the collapse and the graded theorem quantifies over all dimensions including the vacuous encoding (`Main.lean:379-398`). The first two parts remain faithfully represented at `Main.lean:379-386`.

### m02A06

The markdown loses no text: the source asserts uniqueness for every `ε>0` (`problem.tex:1-5`). For the counterexample, suppose `f=0`, `ε=2`, and a `C²` solution tends to zero. Multiplying `-u''+u-2u²=0` by `u'` gives the conserved energy `-(u')²/2+u²/2-(2/3)u³`; decay (and the equation, hence decay of the derivative along the tail) forces this constant to zero. At `x=0`, `u=1`, so `(u'(0))²=1-4/3=-1/3`, impossible. Lean instead assumes `ε<1/(4(1+C))` and proves uniqueness only against bounded competitors (`Main.lean:1985-2001`). Its extra support condition is also stronger than merely being a smooth compactly supported function on the open half-line, because it demands a chosen global smooth zero extension.

### m93J03

The original says “a unique continuous function” for all sufficiently small `|ε|` (`problem.tex:1-4`). At `ε=0`, put `x(t)=b cos t`. The integral becomes `cos t · ∫_{-π}^{π} sin(s+b cos s) ds = 2πJ₁(b) cos t` (the sine coefficient vanishes). Thus every root `b=2πJ₁(b)` is a solution. A nonzero positive root exists: `2πJ₁(b)-b` is positive for sufficiently small `b>0` (derivative at zero is `π-1>0`) and is negative at the first positive zero of `J₁`. Lean explicitly requires both solutions to satisfy the smallness predicate (`Main.lean:689-699`), which is the necessary local uniqueness repair.

### m93J06

The source gives no hypotheses at all in its request to “State and prove” existence/uniqueness (`problem.tex:4-5`), and part (d) assumes only Hölder continuity (`problem.tex:13-15`). For `0<α<1`, `f(t,y)=|y|^α` is Hölder in `y`; from `y(0)=0`, both `y(t)=0` and, for any `c≥0`, `y(t)=0` for `t≤c` and `y(t)=((1-α)(t-c))^{1/(1-α)}` for `t≥c` are `C¹` solutions. Hence there is no well-defined map from initial value to “the” solution. Lean accurately exposes this failure at `Main.lean:1819-1822`, but its other clauses demand a uniform global Lipschitz constant (`Main.lean:1810-1818`) and therefore narrow the usual local theorem and the growth-only global-continuation claim.
