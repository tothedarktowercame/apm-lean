### t01A05

Under the usual convention that “`k`-sheeted” has positive natural `k`, these are sound. For part (c), intersection number must be understood homologically or after transverse perturbation; that is the standard convention rather than a contradiction.

Lean's first conjunct supplies a torus cover only for `0 < k` (lines 73–83), which is a legitimate concrete answer to part (a). Its second conjunct states existence of a top-homology orientation upstairs making a fundamental-class equation true (lines 84–96), rather than the requested formula for every `ω`. Part (c) is expressly deferred (lines 22–31) and absent from the theorem. Moreover, for spaces with no supplied top-homology isomorphism, `∀ o : T01A05Orientation ...` makes the general clause vacuous. Matching the original therefore requires genuine manifold orientation, integration, covering-degree, Poincaré duality/intersection, and all three clauses—not merely restating the current proof.

### t99A04

The source begins with a connected oriented compact surface `X`, but part (a) asks for “closed `1`-forms `η` on `F`” (`problem.tex`, lines 1–12); `F` is never defined, and `problem.md` faithfully retains that defect (lines 12–23). Taking `F=X` is very likely intended. A second convention matters: if compact surfaces may have boundary, the expected zero answer is false. For example, let `X=S¹×[0,1]`, let `f(S¹)=S¹×{1/2}`, and take `η=dθ` normalized to have period `1`. The embedded core separates the annulus into `S¹×[0,1/2)` and `S¹×(1/2,1]`, yet `∫ f*η=1`. Thus “compact” must mean closed/boundaryless (or that hypothesis must be added). On a closed oriented surface a separating simple closed curve is null-homologous, so every closed-form period is zero; for a nonseparating curve, a dual cohomology class gives a nonzero period, and scaling realizes every real value.

Lean acknowledges the undefined `F` (lines 5–12) but its theorem is only the one-variable facts that a periodic derivative integrates to zero and a constant integrates to itself (lines 22–27). There is no surface, embedding, separating predicate, closed differential form, pullback, or connection between the constant integral and a torus counterexample. This needs the named de Rham/Stokes and surface homology theory rather than a small repair.

### t99J03

Lean instead quantifies over compact connected smooth 2-manifolds (lines 120–129); it contains no triangulation datum or theorem relating smooth and triangulated categories, and a proposition cannot itself capture the requested expository proof outline. Yet the Euler formula labels this model with characteristic `2` (lines 61–68). The final theorem also bakes those definitional formulas into its conclusion while the actual classification, orientability dichotomy, and topological invariance remain unproved (lines 131–175). Correct models and substantial surface-classification theory are required; this is over 500 lines even if supporting homology is already present.

### t02A08

The printed data are `Y=S¹×S²`, `Z={p}×S²`, and `f:S²→Y`, with a requested integer “oriented intersection number `I_X(f,Z)`” (`problem.tex`, lines 1–4). `problem.md` reproduces it exactly (lines 19–22). This is not merely a missing transversality hypothesis. In a 3-manifold the expected dimension is
`dim f(S²)+dim Z-dim Y = 2+2-3 = 1`, so the ordinary complementary-dimensional signed intersection count is not defined. A concrete counterexample to the requested setup is `f(q)=(p,q)`: then `f(S²)=Z`, and the intersection is the whole sphere, not a finite signed set and hence not an integer count. The ambient symbol `I_X` is also undefined; it should be `I_Y`.

Lean makes the correct mathematical repair but therefore does not match the original: it defines `Z={y | y.2=q}=S¹×{q}` (lines 37–38), a complementary-dimensional circle, and encodes intersection as the degree of the second projection (lines 62–67). Its fiber maps `(p,g)` (lines 40–43) then have intersection `deg g`, exactly as the corrected problem intends (lines 133–139). The smallest source correction is the one Lean's comment identifies at lines 9–18: replace `{p}×S²` by `S¹×{q}` and `I_X` by `I_Y`.
