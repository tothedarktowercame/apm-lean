### t99A05 — SOUND question, AMBIGUOUS conventions; NEEDS-THEORY
The text asks "Must there be…? Why?" — a yes/no with justification, so sound as a question, but it silently invokes deg₂(f|Z) and I₂(W,Z), whose definitions conventionally need compact Z, Y and a compact (or proper) ambient for I₂ of the requested compact W. Under "X compact" the answer is **yes**: take y regular for f and f|Z; W=f⁻¹(y) is a compact (dim X − dim Y)-submanifold, W ⋔ Z, and W∩Z=(f|Z)⁻¹(y), so I₂(W,Z)=deg₂(f|Z)≠0 — this is the harvested solution.
Literal (no compactness) reading: answer **no**. Counterexample: X=S¹×ℝ (open annulus), Y=S¹, Z=S¹×{0}, f=projection. Then Z is a compact 1-manifold, f|Z=id has deg₂=1≠0. Any compact 1-manifold W⊂X is a finite union of circles; a circle γ has second-coordinate function g:S¹→ℝ whose generic zero set has even cardinality, so every transverse W meets Z={t=0} in an even number of points, hence I₂(W,Z)=0 for all compact W. (Equivalently, the generator of H₁(annulus;𝔽₂) has zero self-intersection.) So the printed universal claim fails without properness. Formalizing either version needs a mod-2 intersection number not in Mathlib. I am confident in the counterexample; slightly less in claiming *every* compact 1-submanifold is a union of circles each homologically a multiple of the core class — that follows from H₁(S¹×ℝ)=ℤ, generated by the core, and parity of intersections computes the mod-2 product, which is zero.

### t99A06 — FALSE as printed; ORIGINAL-DEFECT
Counterexample to the literal statement: X=ℝ (oriented 1-manifold), χ(ℝ)=1≠0 (contractible), and f=ℝ→S¹, f(t)=e^{2πit}, is a local diffeomorphism, hence a submersion. So "χ(X)≠0 ⇒ no submersion X→S¹" is false for noncompact X. Same with any contractible noncompact manifold. Smallest correction: add "compact, without boundary" to X. Under that convention the statement is true and standard: a submersion f lifts the unit vector field of S¹ to a nowhere-zero vector field on X (choose a metric, take the horizontal lift of d𝝿... of the canonical unit field), contradicting Poincaré–Hopf since χ(X)≠0. Formalization additionally needs a manifold Euler characteristic (from homology) and Poincaré–Hopf — neither in Mathlib (repo grep agreed: only algebraic Euler characteristics). So: ORIGINAL-DEFECT, then NEEDS-THEORY (Poincaré–Hopf).

### t98J05 — INCONSISTENT as printed; ORIGINAL-DEFECT + NEEDS-THEORY
The text defines "x ∼ y if and only if y = T(x)". T = rotation by π about the z-axis has fixed points on M (the points where M meets the z-axis — for a genus-2 surface centered on the axis with vertical holes there are two, top and bottom), so this relation is not reflexive or symmetric there: it is **not an equivalence relation**, and the quotient N is not defined as printed. (Also "covering translations (x,y)→(x+m,y+n)" for π:ℝ²→N presumes the claimed torus structure.) Smallest correction: ∼ = equivalence relation generated by T; N is then a branched double cover quotient, a 2-torus (true: genus-2 ÷ ℤ/2 half-turn = torus, Euler 2/2... branched at 2 fixed points: χ = (2 − 2·(1/2·2))/… = 0, orientable ⇒ torus). Part (a): β=p*α with α descending 5dx+dy (which is invariant under the deck group, so it does descend); β closed, and not exact since ∫ over suitable loops gives 5·(winding) + integers ≠ 0 — but p is branched at 2 points, so exactness on M∖(branch points) vs M needs care; the parenthetical hint in the source acknowledges this ("p is a covering map off of a collection of points"). Sound after repair. Part (b) is "draw a curve": formalize as ∃ c, non-null-homotopic ∧ ∫_c β = 0 (e.g. a loop killed by p to a null-homotopic loop in N with zero period). Formalizing needs the entire concrete geometry (genus-2 embedding, quotient smooth structure, form pullbacks): large; NEEDS-THEORY after the source repair.

### lts-derived-induction-lem3 — SOUND; NEEDS-THEORY
The claim "Res_e^G : Sp^G → Sp^e detects equivalences between Borel-equivariant spectra" is definite and true: with Sp^G := Fun(BG, Sp) (Borel model), Res is evaluation at the unique object, evaluation functors reflect isomorphisms (equivalences are pointwise), so Res reflects equivalences — a one-line categorical fact once the foundation exists. No defect in the original. But nothing is formalizable: Mathlib has no spectra category (`Sp`), hence no Fun(BG, Sp). NEEDS-THEORY: stable homotopy/spectra foundations; once present the statement is small.
