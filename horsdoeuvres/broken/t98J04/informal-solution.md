# Informal solution

For (a), orient the two circles. Their linking number is \(\pm1\). If
\(c_2\) were null-homotopic in \(\mathbb R^3\setminus c_1\), a null-homotopy
(after the usual general-position adjustment) would give a spanning disk
disjoint from (c_1). Its algebraic intersection with (c_1) would be zero,
contradicting that this intersection equals the nonzero linking number.

Part (b) depends on a drawing that the TeX source does not include. The verbal
instructions do not determine an isotopy class of a knot in the solid torus:
different choices of how the two stretched ends interlink can produce
different curves. The indicated rigorous method is to lift (T\cong
S^1\times D^2) to \(\mathbb R\times D^2\). A disk bounded by (k) would lift
to a disk bounded by every closed lift of (k). In the intended picture, one
then uses the linked translates of the lifted curve (or a nonzero linking
number with a translated core) to contradict such a disk. Without the missing
diagram or an explicit parametrization, that linking calculation cannot be
identified unambiguously. Thus (a) is proved, while (b)'s precise knot is a
source obstruction rather than something that can safely be guessed.
