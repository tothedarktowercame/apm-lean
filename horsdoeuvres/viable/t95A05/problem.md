## Problem Statement

Raw TeX source:

```tex
Let $X$ be a compact oriented smooth $n$-manifold and let $A$ and $B$
be disjoint closed subsets of $X$. Prove the following:
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{alist}
%------------------------------------------------------------------*
\item There is a compact smooth $n$-(sub)manifold with boundary 
$Y$ in $X$ such that $A\subset Y$ and $\partial Y$ separates $A$ and
$B$. Hint: you may use without proof the well-known theorem that an
arbitrary continuous function $f:X\rightarrow \R$ is
$\epsilon$-approximated by a smooth map $g_{\epsilon}$ for any
$\epsilon>0$ (\ie\ $\forall x\in X: |f(x)-g_{\epsilon}(x)|<\epsilon$).
%------------------------------------------------------------------*
\item Let $\omega$ be any closed $(n-1)$-form on $X\setminus (A\cup
B)$. We can compute a number $N_{\omega}=\int{\partial Y}\omega$ where
$Y$ inherits an orientation from $X$ and $\partial Y$ has the boundary
orientation. Show that $N_{\omega}$ is independent of the choice of
$Y$. Hint: $Y$ can be chosen to lie in an arbitrarily small
neighborhood of $A$. Why?
%------------------------------------------------------------------*
\item Let $f:X\rightarrow X$ be a map with fixed point $x$. If $+1$ is
not an eigenvalue of $df_x:T_x(X)\rightarrow T_x(X)$, then $x$ is a
\emph{Lefschetz fixed point of $f$}, and $f$ is a \emph{Lefschetz map}
if all of its fixed points are Lefschetz. Prove that $X$ is compact
and $f$ is Lefschetz, then $f$ has only finitely many fixed points.
%------------------------------------------------------------------*
\end{alist}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
```
