Let $a<b$ be real numbers and define a function $f_{a,b}$ on $\R$ by
$f_{a,b}(x)=1/\sqrt{(b-a)(x-a)}$ if $a<x<b$ and $f_{a,b}(x)=0$
otherwise.
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{alist}
%------------------------------------------------------------------*
\item Prove that $f_{a,b}$ is integrable and compute
$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{a,b}(x)dx$.
%------------------------------------------------------------------*
\item Prove that there is a function $f$ that is integrable on $\R$
and essentially unbounded on every open interval of reals \ie\ if
$g=f$ a.e. and $\alpha<\beta$ then
$\sup_{\alpha<x<\beta}(g(x))=\infty$. Hint: enumerate the open
intervals with rational end points and apply part (a) and a
convergence theorem involving sums.
%------------------------------------------------------------------*
\end{alist}
